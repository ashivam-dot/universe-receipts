# Universe Receipts: strategy

Last revised 2026-09-29 (Day -2). Evidence for each choice is in `research/`: niche choice in
`research/niche-selection.md`, Shorts mechanics and YPP in `research/shorts-strategy.md`, policy in
`research/policy-factsheet.md`. Web checks made on 2026-09-29 are linked inline.

## Goal and honest odds

- **Owner's goal:** 100,000 subscribers in 100 days (Day 0 is 2026-10-01; Day 100 is 2027-01-09), and YouTube
  Partner Program (YPP) approval on Shorts.
- **What 100k takes:** Shorts convert roughly 0.15–0.36% of viewers to subscribers, so 100k needs about
  28–67 million views in 100 days, or 0.3–0.7 million a day on average (`research/shorts-strategy.md`, section 6).
  Only 1.3% of all channels ever reach 100k, and no verified non-spam channel did it this fast in 2025–26
  (`research/niche-selection.md`, section 4). It would need several breakout Shorts of millions of views each.
- **The best evidence in our niche is sobering.** Cosmic Lens, the fastest verified newcomer in space, went
  from 0 to about 98,800 subscribers in about 9 months with 36 long-form videos, but trackers show it
  gaining only about 200 subscribers in its last tracked 29 days of July 2026
  ([SocialCounts](https://socialcounts.org/youtube-live-subscriber-count/UCjz1107N5-17ZQOz1g6XaOQ),
  [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/channel/cosmiclens-en)): growth came in bursts from a few hits
  (its Pluto video alone passed 2.5M views). A Shorts-heavy space channel, Secrets of Space, has 678 videos
  and 59.2K subscribers after five years, averaging about 2,765 views a video
  ([YT Jobs](https://ytjobs.co/youtube-channel/332256)). Volume alone doesn't do it; breakouts do.
- **What YPP takes:** 1,000 subscribers plus 10 million engaged Shorts views in 90 days. From 2027-02-01
  new applicants need 20 million, and YouTube hasn't said which bar applies to applications still pending
  on that date (`research/policy-factsheet.md`, section 1). Review takes about a month, so we apply the day
  Studio shows eligibility and aim to be in by mid-December (about Day 75). 10M views by then means averaging
  about 130,000 engaged views a day from launch.
- **Our honest cases** (full table in `strategy/PLAN-100.md`):
  - **Base:** about 10M total views and 15,000–25,000 subscribers by Day 100. YPP by mid-December only if a
    breakout lifts the 90-day views to 10M in time.
  - **Stretch (the owner's goal):** 45M+ views and 100,000 subscribers, which needs a Short a week passing
    1M views from about Day 30 on.
  - **Floor:** if no Short passes 50,000 views by Day 30, the format changes before any volume is added.
- **Plan:** maximize the number of well-made, sourced attempts; measure fast; put volume behind whatever
  breaks out (sequels within 48 hours). Never use tactics that put the channel at risk (Hard rules in
  `CHANNEL.md`, and "Never" below).

## Niche and audience

- **Niche:** true, mind-bending space and astronomy stories and facts. One story or fact per Short, and every
  claim carries "receipts": at least two reputable sites behind it, listed in the description.
- **Why space** (`research/niche-selection.md`):
  - Ranked first of six niches: abundant free visuals, low policy risk, and a verified newcomer (Cosmic
    Lens) near 100k in under a year.
  - **Free, real imagery:** NASA, JPL, and USGS imagery is public domain; ESO, ESA/Hubble, ESA/Webb, and
    NOIRLab are CC BY 4.0. Real telescope and mission pictures beat AI renders for trust and for the
    inauthentic-content review. (Main esa.int imagery is CC BY-SA and is never used; `strategy/SCRIPT-RULES.md`.)
  - **Low policy risk:** no health, finance, legal, or political advice; no gore.
  - **Evergreen with news spikes:** a Short about Apophis or Betelgeuse keeps getting views for months, and
    real discoveries give dated hooks.
  - **Revenue, honestly:** space's high long-form RPM (the "Education & Science" median is about $10) barely
    transfers to Shorts. Shorts revenue is a pool split by engaged views and paid at the viewer country's
    rate, about $0.33 per 1,000 US views (`research/shorts-strategy.md`, section 1). Space's advantage is
    that a US-English science audience is valuable if the channel adds long-form later.
  - **Not Days of Odd's niche:** the kit's first channel does strange-but-true history, so the two never compete.
- **Audience:** English-speaking adults 18–44, mostly the US, then the UK, Canada, and Australia; curious
  generalists more than astronomy hobbyists.
- **Not doing:** long-form for now (Shorts only), Hindi, alien or conspiracy framing, "NASA is hiding"
  bait, doom the sources don't state, sci-fi or movie imagery, AI renderings of real objects, present-day politics.

## Format

- **Length:** 40–58 seconds (105–135 spoken words, 6–9 beats; `ytc check` enforces 35–58 s). vidIQ's 331M-Short
  study found 45–59 s gets the most views and reaches 1M views most often; staying under 60 s keeps us clear
  of the rule that blocks longer Shorts with a copyright claim (`research/shorts-strategy.md`, section 3).
- **Structure:** a hook of 12 words or fewer that opens inside the story, context, 2–3 escalating beats, a
  twist, and a last line that runs back into the first so the Short loops. Full rules in `strategy/SCRIPT-RULES.md`.
- **Hook formulas** (rotate them; every hook must be literally true):

| Formula | Example |
|---|---|
| Scale shock | "Put this star where the Sun is, and Saturn is inside it." |
| Real danger, real number | "This dead star could kill you from 1,000 kilometers away." |
| Contradiction | "The first alien planets ever found orbit a dead star." |
| Myth flip | "There is no dark side of the Moon." |
| Number first | "72 seconds. That's how long the signal lasted." |
| Countdown | "In 2029, an asteroid passes closer than our satellites." |
| Mission in trouble | "An oxygen tank exploded on the way to the Moon." |
| Dated news, only when new | "Webb just photographed..." with the date and agency in the description |

  "NASA just found" is used only for a finding dated within about a week, from the agency or a peer-reviewed
  paper. Old discoveries never get "just". Scale comparisons are the most repeated advice for space Shorts
  ([BuzzRAG](https://buzzrag.com/article/solar-system-scale-sizes-distances-explained),
  [FluxNote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/space-youtube-shorts-ideas)), so Scale Breaker leans on familiar
  anchors (Everest, Saturn's orbit, a pixel, a teaspoon).
- **Loop endings:** the last line hands off to the first ("...and it's still out there, because" / "This
  dead star could kill you from 1,000 kilometers away."). A clean loop raises average percentage viewed past
  100% on rewatch.
- **Visuals:** real telescope images (Hubble, Webb, Chandra, ESO), spacecraft and mission photos, mission
  patches, and diagrams; artist's concepts from NASA, ESO, or NOIRLab only for what has never been
  photographed, never called a photo. Designed title cards (a date, a number, a quote) when no picture fits.
  No AI-generated pictures of real objects.
- **Captions:** burned in, 2–3 words at a time, white with the current word highlighted, inside the Shorts
  safe area; the hook as on-screen text (2–6 words) over beat 1.
- **Voice:** Kokoro-82M `af_heart` (Apache-2.0), a warm American narrator, with a blind test against a male
  voice (`am_michael`) in the first weeks. The owner's own voice is the strongest originality signal and the
  fallback if YouTube ever questions the channel (`research/niche-selection.md`, section 2).
- **Audio:** narration at -14 LUFS with synthesized whooshes, pops, and risers. No licensed music: nothing
  gets claimed, and no music share comes out of the revenue pool.

## Series

Named series make the channel look curated rather than templated, and give viewers a reason to follow.

| Series | Promise |
|---|---|
| The Universe Is Terrifying | Real cosmic dangers, with the real numbers that make them scary: voids, magnetars, glass rain |
| Scale Breaker | Sizes and distances made graspable with one familiar comparison |
| Mission Files | The true stories of spacecraft and crews: the triumphs, the mistakes, the ones that didn't come home |
| Space Got Weird | Discoveries so strange they sound made up, and what science actually says about them |
| Near Misses | Asteroids, solar storms, and missions that came far too close, told as probabilities, never prophecies |
| Space Myths, Busted | Things almost everyone believes about space, shown wrong with receipts |

Each series has its playlist (filled by `ytc playlists`), and after Day 21 output shifts toward the series
with the best views per Short and subscribers per 1,000 views.

## Publishing

- **Cadence:** 3 Shorts a day from Day 1. The code steps up by itself to 4 a day from Day 30 and 5 from Day 50,
  only while the last 10 Shorts keep at least 90% of the engaged share of the Shorts before them
  (`auto.PACE_STEPS`); a short stock thins it. Never more than 5 a day. Production keeps up to two weeks of
  finished Shorts ready (42).
- **Times (US Eastern; `publish.SLOTS`):** 8 p.m., 2 p.m., and 10 p.m.; the 4th slot is 5 p.m. and the 5th 11 a.m.
  - Until Oct 31 (US daylight time): 5:30 a.m., 11:30 p.m., and 7:30 a.m. IST (4th 2:30 a.m., 5th 8:30 p.m.).
  - From Nov 1: 6:30 a.m., 12:30 a.m., and 8:30 a.m. IST (4th 3:30 a.m., 5th 9:30 p.m.).
  - After 28 days, check Studio's "When your viewers are on YouTube" report and move slots only with evidence.
- **How:** Buffer's free plan (an official YouTube API partner) publishes from a queue of up to 10 posts,
  kept full by the cloud studio; the rest wait on disk, so a missed run never breaks the schedule.
- **Packaging:**
  - **Title:** 60 characters or fewer, accurate curiosity. Patterns that work in space: "The Terrifying Truth
    About ___" and "___ Is NOT What We Thought" (Cosmic Lens's hits; `research/niche-selection.md`, section 2),
    plus plain claims ("This Star Is Bigger Than Saturn's Orbit"). At most one word in capitals, no emojis,
    and the Short must deliver exactly what the title says.
  - **First frame:** the most striking real image of the subject, with the on-screen hook. Shorts are chosen
    in the feed by the first frame, not a thumbnail.
  - **Description:** one or two sentences, then the sources ("receipts") and image credits, which the
    pipeline appends.
  - **Hashtags:** exactly 3: `#space` plus two specific ones (`#blackhole`, `#nasa`, `#apollo13`).
  - **Settings:** category Science & Technology or Education (whatever the channel's upload defaults set),
    not made for kids, AI disclosure only if a Short ever contains realistic AI imagery (none is planned; a
    non-impersonating synthetic narrator doesn't require it; `research/policy-factsheet.md`, section 3).
- **Anniversaries and sky events:** dated stories (Sputnik on Oct 4, the Orionids on Oct 21, Apollo 12 on
  Nov 14) go out on the date (`strategy/CALENDAR.md`). Voyager 1 reaching one light-day from Earth in
  November 2026 is a timely topic to schedule close to the date NASA gives.

## Originality safeguards (YPP review)

YouTube's July 2026 policy targets templated, mass-produced content and repeated storylines
(`research/shorts-strategy.md`, section 2), and reviewers judge the whole channel. Every Short must be clearly original:

- Each script is written from reputable sources, with a claims table in its folder: two independent sites
  behind every claim, and the sources listed in the description.
- Varied hooks, structures, series, and imagery; no two Shorts share a storyline, and the six series
  keep the channel from looking like one template.
- Real, credited imagery, not AI slop. YouTube removed or demonetized AI-slop channels in 2025 and January
  2026 (`research/shorts-strategy.md`, section 2) and took down an AI-generated channel spreading false 3I/ATLAS
  claims under a scientist's name ([IBTimes](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/youtube-takes-down-ai-generated-3i-atlas-channel-fight-misinformation-1761526)).
- Project files (research, script, render manifest) are kept for every Short to show in any appeal;
  winning appeals showed scripts and timelines.
- No reused footage from other creators, no readings of text we didn't write.

## Growth levers, in order of expected impact

1. **Hooks and first frames:** the first 2 seconds decide "viewed vs swiped away". Shorts under 60% viewed
   rarely take off; winners run 70–90% (`research/shorts-strategy.md`, section 3). Test hook formulas weekly.
2. **Topic demand:** well-known subjects stop more thumbs. The studio already takes each series' most-read
   Wikipedia topic first (`auto.demand`), so Voyager 1, Betelgeuse, and Apollo 13 go before obscure catalog
   numbers; jaw-droppers (Boötes Void, the Oh-My-God particle) supply the surprise.
3. **Breakout follow-through:** when a Short beats the channel's median by 5x, queue 2–4 sequels on the same
   subject or angle within 48 hours (a second magnetar fact, the rest of the Apollo 13 story), pin a comment
   pointing to the related Short, and set its Related video to the next episode. Growth in this niche comes
   in bursts (Cosmic Lens above), so the channel must be ready to feed one.
4. **Series binge:** series playlists, and endings or related-video links that point to the next episode.
   Description and comment links aren't clickable in Shorts; the related-video link is the one bridge we get.
5. **Volume with quality:** about 300 Shorts by Day 100 at 3 a day, up to about 420 if both step-ups trigger,
   all through the quality gate. New channels' first and tenth Shorts perform alike, and the best of ten reaches
   about 6x the median (`research/shorts-strategy.md`, section 3): every reviewed Short is another chance.
6. **Cross-posting (later, optional):** native, watermark-free uploads of the same masters to TikTok,
   Instagram Reels, and Facebook, each uploaded directly (never a watermarked repost, which those platforms
   push down; `research/shorts-strategy.md`, section 5). Starts once the owner creates those accounts; it
   builds reach and gives a second chance to Shorts YouTube passed over.
7. **Collaborations:** YouTube's Collab feature lets up to 10 channels share a Short and reach all their audiences
   (`research/shorts-strategy.md`, section 5). Approach small space and science creators once we have a few hits.
8. **Community:** reply to real comments, and turn good viewer questions ("what's bigger than that?") into episodes.

## KPIs

| Metric | Healthy | Action if below |
|---|---|---|
| Viewed vs swiped away (API stand-in: engaged share) | 60%+ (winners 70–90%) | Rework hooks and first frames |
| Average percentage viewed | 80%+ (loops can pass 100%) | Tighten scripts, cut slow beats, fix the loop seam |
| Subscribers per 1,000 views | 2+ | Strengthen series identity and endings |
| Likes per 100 views | 4+ | Better payoffs |
| Views per Short (7-day median) | Rising week over week; 1,000+ by Day 30 | Change topics or formats |
| Shorts over 100k views | At least 1 by Day 45 for the stretch case | Breakout follow-through, hook experiments |
| Engaged views in the last 90 days | On pace for 10M by mid-December | Decide the YPP path (`strategy/PLAN-100.md`) |

## Weekly experiments

One variable at a time, judged on at least 10 Shorts per arm:

- Hook formula: scale shock vs danger-with-number vs contradiction vs myth flip.
- Length: about 42 vs about 55 seconds.
- On-screen hook text: a number ("1,000 KM") vs the stakes ("IT COULD KILL YOU").
- First frame: the object's real image vs a scale diagram.
- Voice: `af_heart` vs `am_michael` vs the voice-test winner.
- Title style: plain claim vs "The Terrifying Truth About ___" vs "___ Is NOT What We Thought".
- Posting time: the 8 p.m. slot vs 6 p.m. Eastern (only after the 28-day audience report).

## Promotion rules

Allowed: native cross-posts to TikTok, Reels, and Facebook; collaborations with other space and science
creators (YouTube's Collab feature); genuine participation in communities such as r/space and r/astronomy,
following each subreddit's rules, with self-promotion under 10% and never asking for votes
(`research/niche-selection.md`, section 2); replies to comments; asking viewers to subscribe.

Never:

- Bought views, subscribers, likes, or comments; bots; hired promoters who do any of this (their violations count against us).
- Sub-for-sub, engagement pods, rewarded engagement ("comment to win"), comment spam, spammy polls.
- Clickbait lies: a title, hook, or first frame the Short doesn't deliver; "NASA is hiding", "scientists are
  terrified", fake "just found" for old news, doom or alien claims the sources don't make. Egregious clickbait
  is removed, first enforced in India (`research/policy-factsheet.md`, section 8).
- AI slop: AI renderings passed off as real space images, AI voices impersonating scientists, templated
  Shorts with no real research, or readings of someone else's text.
- Reuploads or narration over other creators' footage (Space Engine or Universe Sandbox renders from other
  channels, film clips).

## Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Rejected for "inauthentic content" | Sourced original scripts, six series with distinct storylines, real credited imagery, saved project files, owner voice as fallback |
| A factual error goes viral (space attracts expert commenters) | Two-source rule per claim, precise numbers with "about" kept, hypotheses called hypotheses, quick correction in a pinned comment |
| Space-hype or misinformation association | No alien, conspiracy, or "just found" framing for old news; receipts in every description; science consensus stated plainly (3I/ATLAS is a comet) |
| Copyright claim on an image | Only public domain, CC0, or CC BY (never esa.int's CC BY-SA or movie stills), credits in every description |
| Crowded niche, big incumbents | Stories and "receipts" rather than generic facts; well-known subjects with a surprising twist; series identity |
| Miss the Feb 1, 2027 YPP change | Apply the day Studio shows eligibility; track the 90-day pace from Day 45; decide the path at Day 60 (`strategy/PLAN-100.md`) |
| Views plateau | Weekly experiments; series reweighting at Day 21; format change at Day 30 if the median is under 1,000 |
| Buffer, Cloudinary, or model outage | Up to two weeks of finished Shorts, a queue of 10 in Buffer, `slot_watch` resends, manual Studio upload as last resort |
