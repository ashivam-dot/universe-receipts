# ep036 research: NASA's titanium probe to Venus (Mission Files)

Researched 2026-10-03 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview, gemini-3.5-flash, gemini-3.5-flash-lite) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: Venus is a hellish world where temperatures are hot enough to melt lead, surface pressure is 90 times that of Earth, and the sky is choked with sulfuric acid. Yet, scientists suspect it once harbored vast oceans. To uncover the truth, NASA is returning to Venus with DAVINCI, its first atmospheric entry probe since the 1970s. Set to launch in the late 2020s, DAVINCI will release a beach-ball-sized titanium sphere directly into the toxic, crushing air. For about an hour, this highly engineered sphere will plunge through the clouds, sniffing noble gases and trace elements to trace Venus's climate evolution. Before its final crash-landing, the probe will capture the first high-resolution views of Venus's rugged tesserae mountains since 1982. This daring mission, named after Leonardo da Vinci, could rewrite the history of Earth's mysterious twin.

## Disputed or left out

- The initial proposal in 2015 named Lori Glaze as the Principal Investigator, but by selection in 2021, James B. Garvin was named PI; the script should attribute the initial leadership to Glaze and the active mission to Garvin.
- Early proposals estimated a launch by 2021 and a 63-minute descent, whereas final 2021 plans target late 2020s and generalize the descent to about an hour; the script should use the updated late 2020s timeframe and say about an hour.
