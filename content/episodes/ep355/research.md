# ep355 research: The Comet Panic of 1910 (Space Myths, Busted)

Researched 2026-11-05 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.6-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: In 1910, the return of Halley’s Comet triggered a global panic that science itself accidentally sparked. While tracking the comet, astronomers at Yerkes Observatory used a new tool called a spectrograph to analyze its tail. They discovered cyanogen—a deadly poison gas. When French astronomer Camille Flammarion suggested the gas might "snuff out all life" as Earth passed through the tail, newspapers ignored the scientific caveats about the tail's low density. Hysteria exploded. Families sealed their homes with wet towels, while others bought "comet pills" or gas masks from opportunistic hucksters. Some even held rooftop "comet parties" to toast the apocalypse. On the night of May 18, Earth glided through the tail’s outer streamers. The result? Absolutely nothing. The tail was so rarefied it was effectively a vacuum. Life continued, leaving only the "pill" sellers rich and the sensationalist media looking for their next big scare.
