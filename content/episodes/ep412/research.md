# ep412 research: How One Astronomer Proved the UNIVERSE Is Expanding (Space Got Weird)

Researched 2026-11-13 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview, gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.6-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: Before Edwin Hubble transformed cosmology, most astronomers believed our Milky Way galaxy was the entire universe. Born in Missouri in 1889, Hubble was a standout athlete who studied law at Oxford as a Rhodes Scholar to satisfy his dying father's wishes. He worked as a high school Spanish teacher and served as a U.S. Army major during World War I before devoting himself to astronomy. In 1919, Hubble joined Mount Wilson Observatory, using the world's largest 100-inch Hooker Telescope. By observing Cepheid variable stars, he proved that the Andromeda Nebula was actually an independent galaxy far beyond our own. In 1929, he showed that distant galaxies are moving away from Earth at speeds proportional to their distance. This redshift discovery proved the universe is expanding, prompting Albert Einstein to retract his cosmological constant as his biggest blunder. Today, NASA's premier space telescope carries Hubble's name.

## Disputed or left out

- Hubble's Ph.D. completion year (S1 lists 1921, whereas S2 lists 1914): The script should omit the exact Ph.D. graduation year.
- The claim that fight promoters offered Hubble a shot at world heavyweight champion Jack Johnson (S6 states promoters courted him to fight Johnson, S5 notes promoters 'reportedly' tried to get him to turn pro, while other biographies omit this): The script should omit this detail or explicitly attribute it as 'according to legend'.
- Hubble's initial calculation of the age of the universe (S1 and S6 note his distance errors led to an estimated age of 2 billion years, far below today's accepted 13.8 billion years): The script should focus on the discovery of expansion rather than his precise initial age figure.
- The US Postal Service stamp claim that Hubble would have won the 1953 Nobel Prize in Physics had he not died (S1 notes this assertion is likely false despite his nomination): The script should leave out this stamp citation.
