# ep439 research: The Disappearing Mega-Storm on Neptune (Mission Files)

Researched 2026-11-17 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview, gemini-3.5-flash, gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.6-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: In August 1989, NASA's Voyager 2 executed its final planetary flyby, swooping just thousands of miles above Neptune's north pole. At the outer edge of the solar system, where sunlight is only three percent of what Jupiter receives, scientists expected a frozen, quiet world. Instead, Voyager 2 discovered a violently dynamic atmosphere featuring a giant Earth-sized anticyclone named the Great Dark Spot, whipped by extreme winds. The probe uncovered rings, six previously unknown moons, and a highly offset magnetic field. Hours later, Voyager 2 zipped past Neptune's largest moon, Triton, revealing active ice geysers spewing dark plumes across a world trapped in a backward retrograde orbit. When the Hubble Space Telescope looked back in 1994, Neptune's Great Dark Spot had vanished entirely, proving that the ice giant's wild weather systems are constantly shifting.

## Disputed or left out

- Exact top wind speeds on Neptune: S1 cites winds up to 1,300 mph (2,100 km/h) around storm edges, S3 cites wind speeds up to 1,000 mph, S2 notes winds up to 3 times stronger than Jupiter's, and S5 estimates storm winds around 328 ft/s (100 m/s). Attribute peak wind speeds generally as extreme supersonic speeds between 1,000 and 1,300 mph as measured during the flyby.
- Exact count of Neptune's rings: S2, S4, and S6 state four rings were identified, while S3 describes a system of five rings. Attribute as a system of four to five dark rings.
