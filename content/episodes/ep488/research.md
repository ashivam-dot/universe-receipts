# ep488 research: NASA Caught a Star Swallowing Its Planet (Space Got Weird)

Researched 2026-11-22 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview, gemini-3.5-flash, gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.6-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: In a historic discovery, astronomers caught a star consuming an exoplanet in the Milky Way. The event, named ZTF SLRN-2020, was first detected as an optical outburst by the Zwicky Transient Facility after infrared warming was seen by NEOWISE. Astronomers initially suspected an aging star had expanded into a red giant and engulfed its nearby planet. However, follow-up observations using the James Webb Space Telescope's MIRI and NIRSpec instruments revealed a major twist: the star had not swollen up at all. Instead, it was an unexpanded main-sequence star. Gravitational tidal interactions had caused the Jupiter-sized planet's orbit to slowly decay until it plunged into the star's atmosphere. JWST detected carbon monoxide gas and circumstellar dust around the surviving star, providing the first clear look at the aftermath of a planetary engulfment.

## Disputed or left out

- Distance to the system: NASA JPL states 12,000 light-years while NOIRLab states 13,000 light-years; script should attribute or state approximately 12,000 to 13,000 light-years.
- Onset timing of infrared brightening: Wikipedia cites NEOWISE brightening 7 months prior to optical peak, while NASA JPL states a year prior; script should state several months to a year.
- Initial star classification: Early analysis suggested an expanding red giant star, whereas JWST spectra proved it was an unexpanded main-sequence star; script should contrast the initial red giant hypothesis with JWST's findings.
