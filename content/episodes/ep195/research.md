# ep195 research: The 155-Mile Asteroid with Its Own Rings (Space Got Weird)

Researched 2026-10-19 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.7-flash, gemini-3.8-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: For centuries, astronomers assumed that ring systems were the exclusive glory of colossal gas giants like Saturn and Uranus. Small celestial rocks were thought far too tiny to hold onto a ring. But in June 2013, an international team led by astronomer Felipe Braga-Ribas watched a distant background star pass behind 10199 Chariklo, a 155-mile-wide centaur drifting beyond Saturn. They aimed only to measure the asteroid's size. Instead, seconds before and after the rock occulted the star, the starlight dipped twice in rapid succession. Chariklo was encircled by two dense, icy rings orbiting just 250 miles from its center. The discovery instantly solved a decade-long mystery: Chariklo had dimmed between 1997 and 2008 because its rings had turned edge-on to Earth. The tiny world proved that gargantuan gravity is not required to wear a crown of ice.

## Disputed or left out

- Number of observing sites: ESO (S3), Planetary Society (S5), and NatGeo (S6) mention telescopes at seven locations, while Wikipedia (S2) refers to ten telescopes and thirteen participating institutions/observatories. A script should refer generally to 'telescopes at multiple South American observatories' or specify 'seven successful observing sites.'
- Ring dimensions: Nature (S4) and ESO (S3) report widths of about 7 km and 3 km separated by a 9 km gap, whereas Wikipedia (S1) gives the outer ring width as 0.12 km and a 14 km separation. A script should rely on the primary Nature/ESO peer-reviewed measurements.
- Water ice location: Initial discovery reports (S2, S4) suggested the asteroid body was nearly devoid of water ice and that signatures came almost entirely from the rings, whereas later 2022 JWST data (S1) revealed crystalline water ice on Chariklo's surface itself. The script should attribute early conclusions to initial observations or note the updated JWST surface findings.
