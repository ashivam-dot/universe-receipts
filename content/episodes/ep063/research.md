# ep063 research: The Star on a Collision Course With Our Solar System (Near Misses)

Researched 2026-10-06 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.5-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: In about 1.29 million years, an orange dwarf star named Gliese 710 is projected to plow directly into the outer edges of our solar system's Oort cloud. Currently sitting 62 light-years away in the constellation Serpens, precision data from the Gaia spacecraft reveals this star is heading almost straight toward us at roughly 14.5 kilometers per second. At its closest approach of about 10,520 astronomical units, it will become the brightest object in our night sky besides the Sun, Moon, and Venus, shining with a magnitude of negative 2.7. While the major planets will remain safe and largely undisturbed, its immense gravity will dislodge millions of icy comets, sending a relentless shower of roughly ten naked-eye comets per year streaming into the inner solar system for millions of years.
