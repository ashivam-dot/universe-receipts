# ep349 research: This Galaxy Makes 100 Milky Ways Look Tiny (Scale Breaker)

Researched 2026-11-05 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: Deep in the constellation Lynx, astronomers found a cosmic titan that shatters our expectations of galactic scale. Named Alcyoneus after a mythological Greek giant, this radio galaxy spans an astonishing 16 million light-years across. If you lined up one hundred Milky Ways end to end, they would barely match the length of its colossal plasma plumes. Discovered in 2022 by a team led by Martijn Oei using the LOFAR telescope network, Alcyoneus emits twin jet streams powered by a supermassive black hole at its core. Yet, the real mystery isn't its gargantuan size—it's how it got so big. Despite its record-breaking radio lobes, Alcyoneus is otherwise a surprisingly ordinary elliptical galaxy, with a normal stellar mass and black hole size. Scientists suspect its low-density cosmic web environment might be the secret, letting its jets stretch to unprecedented proportions without resistance.
