# ep482 research: The Cosmic Monster Dragging Our Entire Galaxy (Scale Breaker)

Researched 2026-11-22 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: In 1930, astronomer Harlow Shapley spotted an extraordinary concentration of galaxies in the constellation Centaurus, 650 million light-years away. Decades later, scientists realized this structure—the Shapley Supercluster—was far more colossal than a simple cloud of galaxies. Packing over 8,000 galaxies with a total mass exceeding ten million billion Suns, it is the most massive structure within a billion light-years of Earth. For years, astronomers thought our Milky Way was being pulled primarily by a mysterious region known as the Great Attractor. However, deeper surveys revealed a staggering revelation: the Great Attractor itself, along with the Milky Way and our entire local region of space, is being dragged across the cosmos toward the overwhelming gravitational grip of the Shapley Supercluster.

## Disputed or left out

- Exact number of galaxy clusters in the Shapley Supercluster: S1 mentions 11 core clusters in one paper and 28 clusters in another, while S7 lists 25 major clusters. A script should leave out specific cluster totals or attribute 'containing dozens of major galaxy clusters'.
- Primary cause of peculiar motion: Sources vary on whether the Great Attractor or the Shapley Supercluster is the primary driver, or if dipole repellers contribute. A script should frame it as an interconnected gravitational flow.
