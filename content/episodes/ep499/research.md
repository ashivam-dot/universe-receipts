# ep499 research: The quasar wall that breaks the rules (Scale Breaker)

Researched 2026-11-24 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview, gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.6-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: In 2013, astronomers led by Roger Clowes from the University of Central Lancashire found the Huge-LQG, a structure of 73 quasars in Leo. It spans 4 billion light-years, taking light itself 4 billion years to cross. This size challenges the Cosmological Principle, which assumes the universe looks the same everywhere at large scales. Theory suggests structures shouldn't exceed 370 megaparsecs, but this one hits 1200 megaparsecs. That's 1600 times the distance to Andromeda. Built from quasar nuclei powered by supermassive black holes, it was mapped using Sloan Digital Sky Survey data at redshift 1.3. However, later analysis suggests it might be a statistical false positive. Simulations show random quasar distributions can mimic such clusters. While some data supports its reality, the debate continues. Published in Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society, it remains a scale breaker, testing the limits of cosmic uniformity.

## Disputed or left out

- Whether the Huge-LQG is a real physical structure or a statistical artifact; Clowes et al. claim reality supported by Mg II absorbers, while Nadathur argues it is a false positive from random distribution simulations.
- The specific homogeneity scale limit; sources cite 370 Mpc, 260/h Mpc, or 130 Mpc/h depending on the definition used.
- Whether the structure supersedes others; only one source mentions it was superseded by the Hercules–Corona Borealis Great Wall.
