# ep130 research: The Black Hole Jet Pointed at Earth (The Universe Is Terrifying)

Researched 2026-10-11 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview, gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.7-flash, gemini-3.8-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: On March 28, 2011, NASA's Swift observatory detected a blinding flash in the constellation Draco, cataloged as GRB 110328A. Astronomers initially suspected an ordinary gamma-ray burst, but the blast did not fade away. Instead, it flared repeatedly with intense X-rays over several days. Observations from Swift, the Chandra X-ray Observatory, and Hubble pinpointed the flare directly to the core of a distant galaxy 3.8 billion light-years away. It was not an exploding star. A dormant supermassive black hole had gravitationally ripped an approaching star to shreds. Swirling stellar debris formed an accretion disk, funneling intense magnetic fields into a relativistic particle jet traveling near light speed. By pure cosmic coincidence, this relativistic death ray was aimed directly down the barrel at Earth, boosting its observed brightness by hundreds of times and giving humanity a front-row view of a cosmic shredder.

## Disputed or left out

- Nature of the destroyed progenitor star: S1 notes timing models suggest it was possibly a white dwarf, while mass calculations match a solar-mass star; scripts should simply attribute it as a star without specifying stellar type.
- Initial classification: S2 and S3 treat GRB 110328A as an anomalous GRB or evidence against standard collapse/merger models; scripts should frame the GRB designation as an initial classification that was revised to a tidal disruption event.
