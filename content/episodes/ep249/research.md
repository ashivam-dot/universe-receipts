# ep249 research: Earth’s Long-Lost Second Moon (Space Got Weird)

Researched 2026-10-29 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview, gemini-3.5-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: In late 2024, Earth gained a second moon, but it wasn't a new planet—it was a piece of our old one. Discovered by the ATLAS telescope in South Africa, asteroid 2024 PT5 was captured by Earth's gravity on September 29, remaining a temporary satellite for 57 days. Unlike most space rocks, this bus-sized visitor didn't come from the distant asteroid belt. When astronomers at the Gemini North and IRTF telescopes analyzed its light spectrum, they found a 'smoking gun': its chemical signature perfectly matched lunar rock. This rock was likely blasted off the Moon's surface by a massive impact thousands of years ago. Though it never completed a full orbit before escaping back to its 'Arjuna' asteroid family, it left scientists with a rare chance to study Moon debris up close. It won't stay away forever, though; it’s scheduled to return and become our mini-moon again in 2084.

## Disputed or left out

- Minimoon classification: A 2025 study mentioned in S1 argues it should not be called a minimoon because its orbit is dominantly heliocentric, while S3, S4, S6, and S7 explicitly use the term.
- 2055 capture: S4 suggests that newer data makes a 2055 capture unlikely, while S1, S3, and S6 state it is predicted to occur.
- 1960 capture: S3 and S7 suggest a potential unnoticed capture event in 1960, but this is presented as a reconstruction rather than an observed fact.
