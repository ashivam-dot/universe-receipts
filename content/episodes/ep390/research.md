# ep390 research: The Invisible Star Devouring Cassiopeia's Giant (The Universe Is Terrifying)

Researched 2026-11-08 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview, gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.6-flash, gemini-3.8-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: At the center of the famous W-shaped constellation Cassiopeia sits Gamma Cassiopeiae, a star first identified with peculiar emission lines by Angelo Secchi in 1866. Spinning at extreme speeds, this massive star flings material out from its equator, creating a glowing disk of hydrogen gas that makes its brightness fluctuate. For decades, astronomers were baffled by intense X-ray radiation blazing from the system. Observations by the XRISM space telescope solved the 50-year mystery, proving that an invisible, Earth-sized white dwarf companion is orbiting nearby and devouring gas from the stellar disk. Beyond its violent physics, the star carries a touching human legacy: NASA astronauts nicknamed it 'Navi'—'Ivan' spelled backwards—in 1967 to honor Apollo 1 astronaut Virgil Ivan 'Gus' Grissom. Ancient Chinese astronomers knew it as 'Ce', the whip used to drive a celestial chariot.

## Disputed or left out

- The origin of the name Navi was often mistakenly attributed to Gus Grissom naming the star himself before his death, but evidence from astronaut David Scott shows it was created post-fire in 1967 by the Apollo 9 crew and Dr. Clarence Cleminshaw (attribute: 'contrary to popular belief, the name was coined after Grissom's death by fellow astronauts and Dr. Cleminshaw').
- The exact mechanism behind the star's high-energy X-ray emission was long debated between magnetic disk interactions and white dwarf accretion until XRISM high-resolution data confirmed accretion onto a white dwarf (attribute: 'long debated by astrophysicists, XRISM data confirmed the X-rays come from accretion onto a white dwarf companion').
