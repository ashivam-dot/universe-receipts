# ep201 research: The Largest Star in the Universe Just Shrank (Scale Breaker)

Researched 2026-10-20 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview, gemini-3.5-flash, gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.6-flash, gemini-3.8-flash, gemini-flash-latest) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: In 1860, German astronomers cataloged a dim dot in Scutum as BD-12°5055. Later named UY Scuti, this pulsating red supergiant stunned astronomers in 2012 when measurements from Chile's Very Large Telescope estimated its radius at an astounding 1,708 times that of the Sun. Crowned the largest star known in the universe, it was large enough to swallow the orbit of Jupiter and hold billions of Suns. However, stellar radius calculations depend directly on distance. The 2012 size assumed UY Scuti was roughly 9,500 light-years away. When the European Space Agency's Gaia mission delivered precise parallax measurements, astronomers discovered the star was actually much closer—around 5,900 light-years from Earth. Recalculated with this revised distance in 2023, UY Scuti's radius shrank to approximately 909 solar radii. While still a violent, pulsating behemoth destined for a supernova, the former cosmic titan was officially knocked off its throne.

## Disputed or left out

- Distance and radius values: Historical 2012 calculations placed it at 9,500 light-years and 1,708 solar radii, Gaia DR2 suggested 5,100 light-years and 755 solar radii, while Gaia DR3 calibrations place it at ~5,871–5,900 light-years and ~909 solar radii (or 1,100–1,200 solar radii according to another interpretation). The script should frame this as a scientific progression where improved distance estimates progressively reduced its calculated size.
- Solar system reach: Sources describe UY Scuti reaching past Mars/asteroid belt (for 909 R☉), beyond Jupiter (for 1,700 R☉), or nearing Saturn. The script should explicitly link Jupiter reach to the older 1,708 R☉ estimate and Mars/asteroid belt reach to the newer 909 R☉ figure.
- Earth visibility: S4 claims it can be viewed with binoculars, while S1 and S6 note its apparent magnitude of 8.29 to 10.56 makes it invisible to the naked eye and requires a small telescope. The script should avoid claiming naked-eye visibility.
