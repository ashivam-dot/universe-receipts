# ep162 research: The Shuttle That Survived Seven Hundred Missing Tiles (Near Misses)

Researched 2026-10-17 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.6-flash, gemini-3.7-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: In December 1988, Space Shuttle Atlantis launched on a top-secret Department of Defense mission to deploy a classified spy satellite. Just eighty-five seconds after liftoff, debris from the solid rocket booster struck the orbiter's thermal protection system. When the crew inspected the damage using the robotic arm, they were horrified to find hundreds of chipped tiles and at least one completely missing, exposing bare metal on the ship's belly. Because the mission was classified, the crew had to use a slow, encrypted video feed to transmit images to Mission Control. Ground engineers misread the grainy footage as mere lights and shadows, dismissing the danger. Commander Hoot Gibson privately concluded they were going to die on reentry. Miraculously, Atlantis survived the fiery descent. Upon landing, technicians discovered over seven hundred damaged tiles and a missing tile that had fortuitously sat over a thick aluminum antenna plate, preventing a catastrophic burn-through.
