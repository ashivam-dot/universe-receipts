# ep046 research: The Cosmic Flashes Found by Cold War Satellites (Space Got Weird)

Researched 2026-10-04 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.8-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: In 1967, the U.S. launched Vela satellites to catch the Soviet Union hiding nuclear tests in space. On July 2, sensors detected a flash of gamma radiation, but it didn't match a nuclear bomb's signature. It wasn't a secret weapon; it was the brightest explosion in the universe. This discovery was so sensitive it stayed classified for six years. In 1973, researchers revealed 'Gamma-Ray Bursts'—brief, catastrophic events from deep space. These bursts release more energy in seconds than our Sun will in its entire 10-billion-year life. They occur when massive stars collapse or neutron stars collide, beaming radiation across billions of light-years. We now detect roughly one every day. These 'cosmic beacons' are so powerful they can be seen across the observable universe, all discovered because we were looking for Cold War secrets.

- Scientific consensus on the existence of radio afterglows was disputed until the 1997 discovery of GRB 970508, as noted in S1.
