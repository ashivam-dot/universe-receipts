# ep443 research: The Tiny Rover That Survived Mars (Mission Files)

Researched 2026-11-18 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.7-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: On July 4, 1997, NASA's Mars Pathfinder mission made history by landing the first wheeled vehicle on another planet. Enclosed in a giant protective cushion of airbags, the spacecraft bounced onto the rocky plain of Ares Vallis before opening its petals to release Sojourner. Named after civil rights crusader Sojourner Truth, the tiny 23-pound microrover was designed for a primary mission of just seven to eight days. Instead, it operated on Mars for 85 Earth days, transmitting 550 images and analyzing rocks like Barnacle Bill using its onboard Alpha Proton X-Ray Spectrometer. The lander, designated the Carl Sagan Memorial Station, lost contact on September 27, 1997. Sojourner's findings provided compelling early evidence that ancient Mars was once warm and wet. Today, a twin rover named Marie Curie resides at the Smithsonian, while NASA's Perseverance rover carries Sojourner's iconic silhouette on its deck.

## Disputed or left out

- Sojourner's active sol count varies between sources: S1 states 83 sols (85 Earth days), while S7 states 70 Martian sols (85 Earth days). The script should emphasize 85 Earth days.
- Camera color details differ: S1 and S4 describe two front monochrome cameras and one rear color camera, whereas S7 states two color cameras and one black-and-white camera. A script should simply refer to three onboard cameras.
