# ep225 research: The Planet Made of Diamond (Space Got Weird)

Researched 2026-10-24 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.7-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: In 2011, astronomers discovered a cosmic treasure 4,000 light-years away: a planet made of diamond. This isn’t just a shiny rock; it’s the crystallized corpse of a dead star. Originally a Sun-like star in a binary pair, its neighbor exploded into a pulsar. Over billions of years, the pulsar’s intense gravity siphoned away 99.9% of the star’s mass, stripping it down to its white dwarf core. The remaining carbon and oxygen are so compressed they formed a crystal structure denser than platinum. This "Diamond Planet," known as PSR J1719-1438 b, orbits its host every 130 minutes. The entire system is so compact that the planet’s orbit would fit inside our own Sun. The pulsar itself is a tiny, city-sized sphere only 12 miles wide, yet it spins 10,000 times a minute. It’s a rare survivor of a stellar heist, transformed by extreme pressure into a massive, spacefaring gem.

## Disputed or left out

- One study from 2012 suggested the planet might actually be a 1-kilometer lump of quark matter rather than a white dwarf remnant; the script should focus on the primary diamond planet theory but note it as the leading explanation.
- Whether the planet would actually sparkle like a diamond is speculative; the script should attribute the idea of it 'sparkling' to specific scientist quotes or frame it as a possibility under high-pressure crystallization.
