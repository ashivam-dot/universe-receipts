# ep186 research: The Accidental Glare in the Pale Blue Dot (Scale Breaker)

Researched 2026-10-18 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.6-flash, gemini-3.8-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: On Valentine's Day 1990, NASA's Voyager 1 spacecraft received a historic set of radio commands nearly 4 billion miles from home. Having completed its planetary flybys, the probe turned its narrow-angle camera back toward the inner solar system before shutting down imaging instruments forever. Over the objections of managers who worried aiming toward the Sun might fry the optics, astronomer Carl Sagan and scientist Carolyn Porco championed taking one final family portrait. Among the 60 frames captured was a single image showing Earth suspended in an accidental beam of scattered sunlight bouncing inside the camera optics. In that frame, our entire world spanned just 0.12 of a single pixel—a tiny speck of blue light containing all of human history, inspiring Sagan's unforgettable reminder that Earth is merely a lonely mote of dust in the cosmic dark.

## Disputed or left out

- Pixel dimensions: S7 quotes Candy Hansen recalling the dot appearing 'about two pixels big, three pixels big' on her screen when first discovered, whereas formal NASA photometric analysis in S1, S2, and S4 establishes Earth's true size as 0.12 pixel. The script should explain that Earth occupies just 0.12 of a single pixel physically, though displayed raw pixels or point-spread glare may span more on a monitor.
- Exact distance measurement: Sources vary slightly between 3.7 billion miles (6.055 billion km / 40.47 AU in S1) and 4 billion miles (6.4 billion km in S4; ~4 billion miles in S2, S3, S5, S6, S7). The script should state 'roughly 4 billion miles' or 'about 6 billion kilometers'.
- Year Sagan originally proposed the image: S1 states Sagan proposed the idea in 1980 after the Saturn flyby, while S3 states he submitted his proposal to the project in 1981. The script should say 'in the early 1980s after passing Saturn'.
