# ep118 research: The Moon That Was Smashed to Pieces (Space Got Weird)

Researched 2026-10-10 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview, gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.7-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: When NASA's Voyager 2 reached Uranus in 1986, scientists expected the moon Miranda to be a boring, frozen orb. Instead, they discovered a 'Frankenstein' world that looked like it was smashed to bits and glued back together by a toddler. This cosmic patchwork features mismatched geological blocks that make no sense sitting next to each other. Miranda boasts Verona Rupes, a cliff so massive it is 12 times deeper than Earth's Grand Canyon. One leading theory suggests the moon was completely shattered by catastrophic impacts up to five times, with the pieces reassembling into a chaotic pile of ice and rock. Because Uranus orbits on its side, Miranda endures extreme 42-year seasons of total darkness or constant light. We have only ever seen its southern half; the rest remains a mystery. This tiny moon, with a surface area barely larger than Texas, remains the solar system's weirdest jigsaw puzzle.

## Disputed or left out

- Height of Verona Rupes: Sources vary wildly, ranging from 3 miles (S7) to 12 miles (S3, S6); S1 provides both 12 miles and 3-6 miles in different sections. The script should use 'up to 12 miles' or attribute the height as an estimate.
- Formation of the coronae: Some sources favor the 'shattered and reassembled' theory (S1, S2, S3), while newer studies favor tidal heating and ice upwelling (S1, S5, S6, S7). The script should present both as competing scientific hypotheses.
