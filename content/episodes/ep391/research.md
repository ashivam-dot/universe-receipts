# ep391 research: The Interstellar Message Aimed at Nothing (Space Got Weird)

Researched 2026-11-09 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.5-flash, gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.6-flash, gemini-3.8-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: On November 16, 1974, astronomers gathered in Puerto Rico to celebrate upgrades to the giant Arecibo radio telescope by beaming humanity's first intentional interstellar radio message into deep space. Designed primarily by astronomer Frank Drake with assistance from Carl Sagan and Cornell researchers, the broadcast lasted under three minutes. It transmitted exactly 1,679 binary bits at 2,380 megahertz. Formatted into a grid of 73 by 23 characters, the decoded ones and zeros formed a graphic depicting numbers, DNA chemistry, the double helix, a human stick figure, the solar system with Earth offset, and the telescope itself. Drake aimed the signal at Messier 13, a globular star cluster 25,000 light-years away. However, the message was purely a symbolic tech demonstration: traveling at light speed, the signal will take 25,000 years to arrive, by which time the cluster will have shifted along its orbit.

## Disputed or left out

- Whether the message will completely miss M13 or hit near its center: S5 and S6 state the cluster will have moved well out of the way, S2 notes most stars will have moved out of the beam, while S1 argues proper motion is small enough that the beam will still arrive near the center. Scripts should attribute this: 'while the cluster moves along its galactic orbit, astronomers disagree on whether the expanding beam will still clip its center.'
- Whether Arecibo was the very first message sent into space: S2 and S3 note that earlier radar pulses and the 1962 Soviet Morse message bounced off Venus, but Arecibo was the first intentional transmission sent to an interstellar star cluster.
