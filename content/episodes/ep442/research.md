# ep442 research: Why the Moon Smells Like Spent Gunpowder (Scale Breaker)

Researched 2026-11-18 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview, gemini-3.5-flash, gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.7-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: Born roughly 4.5 billion years ago when a Mars-sized protoplanet collided with early Earth, the Moon is far stranger than the quiet silver disk in our sky. It is locked in synchronous rotation, matching its 27-day spin to its orbit and showing Earth only one face, though its far side receives just as much sunlight as the near side. While its gravitational pull drives Earth's ocean tides, the Moon is slowly spiraling away by about an inch each year. Down on its surface, where gravity is just one-sixth of Earth's, an abrasive dust layer called regolith coats the ground. Solar radiation charges this razor-sharp dust, causing it to levitate across the landscape. The dust smells like burnt gunpowder and caused allergic reactions in all twelve astronauts who walked on the lunar surface. Yet beneath this harsh vacuum exosphere, permanently shadowed craters harbor ancient water ice.

## Disputed or left out

- Exact age of lunar formation: S1 cites isotopic dating indicating 4.35 billion years ago, whereas S2 states about 4.5 billion years ago; a script should state 'between 4.35 and 4.5 billion years ago'.
- Crust thickness values: S1 gives an average crustal thickness of about 50 km, while S2 reports 70 km on the near side and 150 km on the far side; a script should cite crust thickness as variable and model-dependent.
- Annual recession rate: S1 measures the retreat as 38 mm (1.5 in) per year from laser ranging, while S2 approximates it as 'about an inch'; a script should phrase it as 'roughly an inch to an inch and a half each year'.
