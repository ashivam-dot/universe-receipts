# ep450 research: How Scientists Photographed a Monster Black Hole (Scale Breaker)

Researched 2026-11-19 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.6-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: On April 10, 2019, astronomers revealed humanity's first direct image of a black hole: the supermassive beast M87*, lurking 55 million light-years away in the constellation Virgo. Packing 6.5 billion times the mass of the Sun into a single cosmic abyss, its event horizon spans nearly 40 billion kilometers—easily dwarfing our entire solar system. Because black holes swallow all light, the object itself is invisible. Instead, telescopes captured its dark shadow silhouetted against a swirling, incandescent ring of matter whipped to near light-speed. Capturing this cosmic silhouette required the Event Horizon Telescope, a global network of eight radio observatories synchronized with atomic clocks to effectively turn planet Earth into one giant virtual dish. The resulting image confirmed Albert Einstein's general theory of relativity under the universe's most extreme gravitational conditions, proving that what once seemed mathematically impossible could finally be seen.

## Disputed or left out

- Distance to Messier 87: Sources S1 and S7 cite approximately 53 to 53.5 million light-years, while S3 and S4 cite 55 million light-years. Scripts should state 'roughly 53 to 55 million light-years'.
- Displacement of the black hole from the galactic center: A 2010 study suggested an offset of 23 light-years, but subsequent 2011 and 2018 studies determined this was an artifact of variable jet brightness. Scripts should omit this debunked offset.
- Photon ring detection claim: An August 2022 remastered analysis claimed to resolve the fundamental photon ring signature of M87*, but this result was formally disputed by other astronomers. Scripts should attribute or omit this claim.
