# ep106 research: The Missing Hyphen That Wasn't a Hyphen (Mission Files)

Researched 2026-10-09 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.5-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: On July 22, 1962, NASA launched Mariner 1 toward Venus aboard an Atlas-Agena rocket, aiming for humanity's first planetary flyby. Less than five minutes into the flight, the rocket began fishtailing erratically. Heading dangerously toward Atlantic shipping lanes, it was blown apart by the range safety officer at 293 seconds, just six seconds before booster separation. The culprit became legendary when Arthur C. Clarke dubbed it the most expensive hyphen in history. But it was not actually a hyphen. Transcribers copying handwritten flight equations into computer code had omitted a single mathematical overbar above the letter R. That overbar instructed the ground computer to smooth out noisy radar data. When an onboard beacon briefly glitched, the guidance software mistook minor jitter for extreme velocity changes, sending violent corrections that wrecked the flight. Fortunately, NASA launched identical twin Mariner 2 five weeks later with corrected code, making history at Venus.

## Disputed or left out

- Hyphen vs. Overbar: Popular media and Arthur C. Clarke claimed a missing hyphen caused the explosion, but technical investigations proved it was a missing mathematical overbar over the letter R denoting a smoothed value; scripts should present the hyphen label as a popular misnomer.
- Destruct timing: S2 states T+294.5 seconds, while S3, S5, S6, and S7 state 293 seconds; scripts should round to approximately 293 seconds or under five minutes.
- Necessity of destruction: Program director Jack James believed destroying the rocket was unnecessary because it would have fallen harmlessly in the open ocean; scripts should attribute this claim specifically to James.
