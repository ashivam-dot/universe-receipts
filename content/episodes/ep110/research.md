# ep110 research: The Lost Space Forest NASA Forgot Existed (Space Got Weird)

Researched 2026-10-09 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.5-flash, gemini-3.8-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: In 1971, Apollo 14 astronaut Stuart Roosa carried hundreds of tree seeds in his personal kit while orbiting the Moon. This joint NASA and Forest Service project aimed to study how space travel affected plant life. Back on Earth, the seed canister accidentally burst inside a decontamination vacuum chamber, but the Forest Service successfully germinated them anyway. In 1975 and 1976, hundreds of these Moon trees were planted across the U.S. for the bicentennial, but NASA kept no records of their locations. By 1996, the trees were completely forgotten. That year, third-grade teacher Joan Goble and her class found a sycamore labeled Moon tree at an Indiana camp. When Goble emailed NASA, archivist Dave Williams and his colleagues had no idea what she was talking about. Williams dug through old archives, built a public tracking website, and sparked a nationwide hunt that continues to find these long-lost space monuments.

## Disputed or left out

- The exact number of seeds Roosa carried is disputed, with sources claiming either about 500 or more than 2,000 seeds; a script should refer generally to 'hundreds of seeds' to avoid contradiction.
- The details about an initial, failed germination attempt in Houston due to inadequate facilities are only mentioned in S2, while other sources only mention successful germination at Forest Service stations; a script should focus on the successful germination at the Forest Service stations.
