# ep352 research: The Probe That Fell Into Venus (Mission Files)

Researched 2026-11-04 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: In 1967, the Soviet Union achieved a historic interplanetary first when the Venera 4 probe plunged into the crushing skies of Venus. As it hit the planet's thick atmosphere, the capsule experienced a staggering deceleration of 300 Gs while its heat shield endured temperatures soaring up to 11,000 degrees Celsius. Descending slowly by parachute, Venera 4 became the very first spacecraft to perform in-situ analysis of another planet's environment, transmitting direct measurements back to Earth every 48 seconds. It revealed that Venus possessed an extremely dense atmosphere composed overwhelmingly of carbon dioxide. However, the probe's journey ended abruptly when it was crushed by immense atmospheric pressure while still 25 kilometers above the surface. At the time, scientists initially miscalculated the surface pressure, but combined data later proved Venus was a scorching hellscape.

## Disputed or left out

- Whether the probe reached the surface intact: initial Soviet announcements claimed it reached the surface, but later American Mariner 5 data and re-analysis proved the hull was crushed at high pressure before landing, which the script should attribute as an initial misconception later corrected.
