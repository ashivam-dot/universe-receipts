# ep515 research: The NASA Lander That Cut Its Own Engines (Mission Files)

Researched 2026-11-28 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.6-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: In December 1999, NASA's Mars Polar Lander arrived at Mars to execute the first touchdown near the Red Planet's south pole. Equipped with a robotic digging arm, microprobes, and the first Mars microphone, the lander aimed to study polar ice and climate. However, under NASA's strict budget caps, project managers made a risky call to omit real-time telemetry during entry, descent, and landing. On December 3, the lander entered the atmosphere and vanished. When silence persisted, an investigation reconstructed the tragic blunder: as the spacecraft descended on a parachute, deploying its landing legs created a sharp mechanical vibration. The flight software misread that brief signal as contact with the Martian surface. The moment the touchdown logic activated 40 meters above the ground, the computer cut the descent engines, sending the $165 million probe free-falling to its destruction.

## Disputed or left out

- S3 states the lander carried a pair of microphones, whereas S1, S2, and S6 describe a single Mars Microphone mounted inside the LIDAR instrument. Script should stick to a single Mars Microphone provided by The Planetary Society.
- Candidate wreckage spotted in 2005 Mars Global Surveyor imagery was later disproved as terrain illumination and image noise in October 2005. Script should state the lander remains unfound.
