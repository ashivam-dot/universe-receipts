# ep421 research: How NASA Slung Voyager 1 Into Deep Space (Mission Files)

Researched 2026-11-14 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview, gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.6-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: In September 1977, NASA launched Voyager 1 from Cape Canaveral aboard a Titan III E rocket on a landmark mission to explore the outer Solar System. Constructed by the Jet Propulsion Laboratory, the spacecraft completed successful flybys of Jupiter in 1979 and Saturn in 1980. At Saturn, mission planners faced a pivotal decision: trajectory options allowed visiting Pluto or making a close flyby of Titan, Saturn's largest moon discovered by Christiaan Huygens in 1655. Because Titan was known to possess a dense, nitrogen-rich atmosphere, scientists prioritized Titan. On November 12, 1980, Voyager 1 passed within 124,000 kilometers of Saturn's cloud tops before sweeping past Titan. Titan's gravitational effect flung Voyager 1 below Saturn's south pole and out of the ecliptic plane, ending its planetary reconnaissance and placing it on an irreversible path into interstellar space.

## Disputed or left out

- Subsurface ocean on Titan: Earlier analysis of Cassini data suggested Titan possesses a subsurface liquid ocean, whereas a 2025 Nature analysis argues its strong tidal dissipation precludes a global ocean, indicating a slushy high-pressure ice layer instead. The script should attribute this: 'While earlier models proposed an underground liquid ocean on Titan, recent analysis suggests it may consist of warm, slushy high-pressure ice.'
- Apparent diameter of Titan: Prior to Voyager 1, Titan was believed to be larger than Jupiter's moon Ganymede because its dense atmospheric haze increased its apparent visual size. The script should attribute: 'Before Voyager 1 arrived, astronomers believed Titan was the Solar System's largest moon due to its thick atmospheric haze.'
