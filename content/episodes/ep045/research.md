# ep045 research: Why NASA Killed a $3B Spacecraft (Mission Files)

Researched 2026-10-04 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.5-flash, gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.8-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: In 2017, after two decades in space, NASA's Cassini spacecraft met a fiery, intentional end. Rather than letting it float dead in space, scientists commanded it to plunge directly into Saturn's crushing atmosphere. The reason? Cassini had discovered that Saturn's moons, Enceladus and Titan, harbor subsurface oceans and chemistry that could support alien life. With Cassini's rocket fuel nearly depleted, scientists feared the probe might someday crash into these pristine moons, contaminating them with hardy stowaway Earth microbes. During its final orbit, a daredevil phase called the "Grand Finale," Cassini dove between Saturn and its rings. As it plunged into Saturn's atmosphere on September 15, 2017, it kept its antenna pointed at Earth, transmitting priceless data until the very end. It fought Saturn's winds, lasting 30 seconds longer than expected, before burning up like a meteor.

## Disputed or left out

- The exact amount and composition of plutonium fuel on board is reported slightly differently across sources (S1 states 33 kg of plutonium-238, S2 states 32.7 kg of nuclear fuel containing 28.3 kg of pure plutonium, and S6 states 32.7 kg of plutonium). A script should refer to it generally as about 33 kilograms or 72 pounds of plutonium.
- The hypothetical Earth-impact cancer death toll calculation is disputed, with S1 noting an incorrect calculation of 500,000 deaths found in some external accounts, compared to NASA's estimate of up to 5,000 deaths. The script should stick to the official 5,000 maximum estimate or avoid the hypothetical scenario entirely.
