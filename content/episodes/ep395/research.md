# ep395 research: The Moon's First Rover That Never Gave Up (Space Got Weird)

Researched 2026-11-10 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.6-flash, gemini-3.8-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: In November 1970, the Soviet Union soft-landed Luna 17 in the Moon's Sea of Rains, rolling out Lunokhod 1—the first remote-controlled rover on another world. Shaped like an eight-wheeled bathtub with a convex lid, it was designed to survive just three lunar days. Instead, powered by solar cells by day and warmed by a radioactive polonium core through freezing two-week nights, Lunokhod 1 survived eleven lunar days. Over nearly eleven months, it traversed over six miles, beamed back 20,000 television pictures, and probed hundreds of soil sites. Then, in the autumn of 1971, contact went silent. For almost four decades, the lost robot sat forgotten in the lunar dust. But in 2010, NASA orbiter photos pinpointed its coordinates. When physicists in New Mexico aimed a telescope laser at its French-built reflector, Lunokhod 1 fired back 2,000 photons on the very first try—blazing five times brighter than newer reflectors.

## Disputed or left out

- Soviet folklore alleged the rover was piloted by an internal dwarf; scripts should omit this or treat it purely as an urban legend.
- Exact distance traversed varies slightly between 10.5 km, 10.54 km, and 'almost 7 miles'; scripts should safely phrase it as 'more than 6 miles' or 'over 10 kilometers'.
- Mission operational span is variously stated as 321 Earth days, 322 Earth days, or 10 to 11 months; scripts should describe it as 'nearly 11 months' or attribute precise day counts.
