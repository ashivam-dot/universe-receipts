# ep253 research: The 45-Year Cover-Up of Space Dog Laika (Mission Files)

Researched 2026-10-29 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.5-flash, gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.6-flash, gemini-3.8-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: In October 1957, Soviet leader Nikita Khrushchev demanded a dramatic space spectacular to celebrate the 40th anniversary of the Bolshevik Revolution in under four weeks. Rocket designer Sergei Korolev rushed to assemble Sputnik 2, installing a stray Moscow street dog named Laika inside a hastily built cabin. Because re-entry technology did not exist, the mission was designed to be one-way. Scientists trained Laika in progressively smaller cages and monitored her vital signs during launch on November 3, 1957. For decades, official Soviet accounts claimed Laika survived several days in orbit before being painlessly euthanized. However, in 2002, mission scientist Dimitri Malashenkov revealed the truth: a failure in thermal control caused cabin temperatures to soar, killing Laika within five to seven hours from panic and overheating on her fourth orbit. Sputnik 2 burned up during re-entry on April 14, 1958, after 2,570 orbits.

## Disputed or left out

- Laika's original name: Different sources report names including Kudryavka, Limonchik, Malyshka, and Zhuchka. Script should note she was originally called Kudryavka before being renamed Laika.
- Electro-cardio signals on day two: A declassified 1957 letter suggested heart activity on day two, but historians point out orbital tracking gaps and thermal breakdown make this unreliable. Script should rely on the 2002 Malashenkov disclosure that she died on orbit four.
- Initial launch time: Soviet launch records vary between 05:30:42 and 07:22 Moscow Time. Script should avoid giving a specific launch time minute.
