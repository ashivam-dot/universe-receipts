# ep119 research: The City-Killer Hiding in the Sun (Near Misses)

Researched 2026-10-10 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.7-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: On July 13, 2023, a 200-foot-wide asteroid designated 2023 NT1 swept past Earth at just one-quarter of the distance to the Moon. Large enough to cause catastrophic local devastation, the space rock flew completely undetected past our planet. It approached from the daytime sky, concealed entirely within the intense glare of the Sun. Early-warning systems remained blind until two days after the closest encounter, when an ATLAS telescope in South Africa finally spotted the asteroid leaving our cosmic neighborhood. The close call echoed the 2013 Chelyabinsk meteor, which also sneaked in from the solar blind spot and injured roughly 1,500 people. While 2023 NT1 missed us, the incident prompted astronomers to model emergency defense proposals, such as hypervelocity interceptor arrays designed to pulverize surprise impactors on less than twenty-four hours of warning.

## Disputed or left out

- Asteroid velocity: S2 quotes NASA estimating 53,000 mph (86,000 km/h), S5 reports 25,000 mph, and S7 calculates 11.27 km/s at closest approach and 15.59 km/s entry velocity; scripts should omit exact speed figures or attribute them specifically to orbital models.
- Exact diameter: Media reports state up to 60 meters (200 feet), while technical analysis in S7 gives a most probable diameter of 34 meters (range 26-58 meters) and S1 notes approximately 30 meters; scripts should present the measurement as a range up to 200 feet.
- Chelyabinsk casualty figures: S2, S3, and S4 cite roughly 1,500 people injured, while S5 reports over 1,600; scripts should attribute the count broadly as around 1,500 people.
