# ep369 research: Saving Apollo 13 With Duct Tape (Near Misses)

Researched 2026-11-06 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview, gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.6-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: On April 11, 1970, Apollo 13 launched toward the Moon carrying Jim Lovell, Fred Haise, and Jack Swigert. Nearly 56 hours into the flight, an oxygen tank exploded in the service module, destroying the oxygen supply and cutting off primary electrical power. Stranded 200,000 miles from Earth, the crew was forced to abandon their lunar landing and use the Lunar Module Aquarius as a makeshift lifeboat. Because the lander was built for two people, carbon dioxide quickly built up to dangerous levels. The Command Module had spare filter canisters, but they were the wrong shape. Mission Control engineered a crude adapter using plastic bags, cardstock, spacesuit hoses, and duct tape, radioing instructions up to space. The crew built the contraption, immediately dropping carbon dioxide levels. In freezing cabin temperatures, the astronauts endured the long trip home, splashing down safely in the Pacific Ocean on April 17 before 40 million television viewers.

## Disputed or left out

- Use of a sock in the filter: Prompts or legends mention a sock, but NASA sources specify plastic covers, cardstock, spacesuit hoses, and duct tape. Script should leave out the sock detail.
- Famous quote phrasing: Popular media quotes 'Houston, we have a problem', but transcripts show the actual radio message was 'Houston, we've had a problem'. Script should clarify or present the exact historical quote.
- Fred Haise's illness detail: Source S1 identifies it as a urinary tract infection, while S3 describes it as a kidney infection. Script should refer to it generally as an infection resulting from severe water rationing.
