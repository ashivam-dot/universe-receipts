# ep512 research: How a Space Ban Created a New Station (Mission Files)

Researched 2026-11-27 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview, gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.6-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: When US law barred China from participating in the International Space Station, China responded by building its own modular space station in orbit. After testing rendezvous and refueling techniques on precursor labs Tiangong-1 and Tiangong-2, China launched the Tianhe core module in April 2021. The first crew arrived aboard Shenzhou 12 in June 2021, and the addition of the Wentian and Mengtian experiment modules in 2022 completed the station. Orbiting roughly 400 kilometers above Earth, Tiangong permanently hosts three astronauts for six-month missions, expanding to six during crew handovers. Despite political barriers, Tiangong hosts research from 17 countries and previously included joint survival training with European Space Agency astronauts. To supercharge its research, China built Tiangong to companion with the Xuntian space telescope, which boasts a view 300 times wider than Hubble and is designed to dock with Tiangong for refueling and maintenance.
