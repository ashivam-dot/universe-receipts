# ep503 research: Why Space Won't Instantly Freeze You (Space Myths, Busted)

Researched 2026-11-24 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview, gemini-3.1-flash-lite, gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.6-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: Popular depictions suggest outer space causes instant freezing and explosive bursting, but real space exposure is vastly different. Heat transfer in a vacuum relies strictly on radiation without conduction or convection, meaning heat leaves the human body slowly and there is no danger of freezing instantly. Furthermore, human skin and tissues are strong enough to prevent the body from exploding. The immediate threat in a vacuum is hypoxia: as lungs strip oxygen from the blood, a person loses consciousness in roughly 14 to 15 seconds. During a 1960s NASA vacuum chamber test, engineer Jim LeBlanc lost suit pressure and recalled the saliva on his tongue bubbling before blacking out. While short vacuum exposure can be survived, prolonged spaceflight damages the human body, causing bone loss, muscle atrophy, vision impairment, and potential brain and gastrointestinal damage from deep-space radiation.
