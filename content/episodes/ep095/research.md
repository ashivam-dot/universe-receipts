# ep095 research: The Coldest Place in the Natural Universe (Scale Breaker)

Researched 2026-10-09 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.8-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: Deep in the constellation Centaurus, 5,000 light-years away, lies the Boomerang Nebula—the only place in the natural universe known to be colder than the afterglow of the Big Bang. While most of space is warmed to 2.7 Kelvin by Cosmic Microwave Background radiation, the Boomerang Nebula is a bone-chilling 1 Kelvin (-272°C). This 'cosmic refrigerator' is a young planetary nebula formed by a dying red giant star. The star is shedding its mass 100 times faster than similar stars, blasting gas into the void at speeds reaching 500,000 kilometers per hour. This rapid expansion causes adiabatic cooling, sucking heat away faster than the surrounding universe can replenish it. Originally named in 1980 for its curved appearance in ground-based telescopes, Hubble’s sharper eyes later revealed its true form: a symmetrical bow-tie. It is a fleeting moment in stellar death where the expansion is so violent it breaks the temperature scales of the cosmos.

## Disputed or left out

- The exact naming year is cited as 1980 by most sources, but one source notes naming-related observations in July 1979; the script should use 'around 1980'.
- Gas expansion speed varies between 164 km/s and 500,000 km/h across sources; the script should say 'hundreds of thousands of kilometers per hour'.
- The mass loss rate is cited as 1/1000th solar mass per year in some sources and 'one Sun's worth since 500 AD' in another; the script should focus on it being '100 times faster than similar stars'.
- The central star's temperature is estimated between 6000K and 7000K; this detail should be omitted as it is not central to the 'coldest place' narrative.
