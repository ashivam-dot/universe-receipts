# ep396 research: The Meteor Storm That Created Space Science (Space Got Weird)

Researched 2026-11-10 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview, gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.7-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: On the night of November 12-13, 1833, the night sky over North America exploded into a fiery blizzard. Up to 240,000 meteors fell over nine hours, terrifying onlookers who feared the end of the world. At the time, mainstream scientists believed shooting stars were atmospheric phenomena like lightning. But Yale professor Denison Olmsted noticed something revolutionary: every meteor appeared to radiate from a single stationary point in the constellation Leo. By collecting accounts from across the continent, Olmsted proved that meteors originate from a cloud of cosmic debris in outer space. Astronomers later connected the Leonids to comet 55P/Tempel-Tuttle, which leaves behind dense dust trails every 33 years. What began as an apocalyptic nightmare transformed human understanding of space, establishing the modern science of meteors.

## Disputed or left out

- Exact peak hourly meteor count for the 1833 storm varies between sources (50,000 to 240,000 meteors per hour); a script should state 'an estimated 100,000 to over 200,000 meteors per hour'.
- Whether an earlier storm occurred in Cumana in 1766 is noted as a rumour; script should leave out or attribute as 'according to rumor'.
- The 1933 absence was blamed by some on Uranus perturbations or bad weather; script should focus on the confirmed Jupiter perturbations.
