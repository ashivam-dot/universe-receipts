Researched 2026-11-14 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.6-flash, gemini-3.8-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: In 1998, the launch of the Zarya module marked the birth of humanity's largest orbital outpost: the International Space Station. Built by fifteen nations and five space agencies, this football-field-sized megastructure has an internal pressurized volume comparable to a Boeing 747 and has hosted continuous human life since November 2000. Orbiting 250 miles above Earth, it circles the planet every 90 minutes. Yet low Earth orbit is an unforgiving environment. Decades of intense thermal cycles, atmospheric drag, and structural aging are taking their toll. While commercial ventures from Axiom Space and Russia's planned replacement station prepare to spin off, the ISS itself cannot remain in orbit indefinitely. Abandoning it would leave a 400-ton behemoth to tumble uncontrollably back to Earth. Instead, NASA plans to deorbit the station around 2030, using a dedicated U.S. Deorbit Vehicle to guide the blazing complex down into the remote South Pacific Ocean.

## Disputed or left out

- Total station program cost: Wikipedia cites approximately $150 billion USD, whereas Space.com cites $100 billion. The script should cite a range or attribute with 'costing over $100 billion.'
- Deorbit versus orbit raising: NASA and Planetary Society analyze boosting the ISS to a graveyard orbit as cost-prohibitive and impractical, while Wikipedia notes that proposals to park the station in a higher orbit have gained congressional interest. The script should present ocean deorbit as the agency baseline while noting alternative proposals have been debated.
- Assembly flight tally: NASA states 42 total assembly flights (37 Space Shuttle, 5 Russian rockets), whereas Wikipedia mentions over 40 assembly flights with 36 Space Shuttle flights. The script should use the broader phrase 'over 40 assembly flights.'
