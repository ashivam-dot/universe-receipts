# ep127 research: The Supersonic Impostor in Stephan's Quintet (Space Got Weird)

Researched 2026-10-11 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview, gemini-3.5-flash, gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.7-flash, gemini-3.8-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: Discovered in 1877, Stephan's Quintet in Pegasus looks like a tight squad of five interacting galaxies. But space is full of photobombers. Modern telescopes revealed that one of them, NGC 7320, is actually a foreground interloper sitting just thirty-nine million light-years away, while the other four are parked a staggering three hundred million light-years out. To make matters crazier, another intruder named NGC 7318B is currently plowing straight into the group at millions of miles per hour. This supersonic impact generates a colossal shock wave bigger than the entire Milky Way, tearing apart gas clouds and triggering frantic bursts of star birth. Even Hollywood got in on the act, using an old image of the quintet to represent glowing angels in the 1946 holiday classic It's a Wonderful Life.
