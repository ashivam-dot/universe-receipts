# ep033 research: The Cold War Treaty That Banned Nuclear Weapons in Space (Space Got Weird)

Researched 2026-10-03 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.5-flash, gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.6-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: During the height of the Cold War, the United States and the Soviet Union were locked in a tense space race. But instead of letting their conflict explode into the cosmos, they negotiated a groundbreaking agreement: the 1967 Outer Space Treaty. Opened for signature in Washington, London, and Moscow, this foundation of space law legally designated outer space as the 'province of all mankind.' It strictly bans nations from deploying nuclear weapons or other weapons of mass destruction in Earth's orbit and completely forbids claiming sovereignty over the Moon or any celestial body. No country can own the Moon, regardless of how many flags are planted there. However, the treaty has fascinating gaps: it allows conventional weapons in space, and it doesn't clearly regulate modern asteroid and commercial mining. This Cold War relic remains the cornerstone of space law today, keeping the heavens a zone of peace.

## Disputed or left out

- The exact number of member states: S5 mentions 115 states-parties as of June 2024, whereas S1 and S6 mention 118 parties by late 2025. A script should note the timeline difference.
- The legality of commercial space mining: S3 asserts that the US space mining law violates the Outer Space Treaty, while S1 and S6 present it as an open debate and legal ambiguity. A script should present space mining as a source of legal debate rather than a definitive violation.
