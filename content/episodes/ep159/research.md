# ep159 research: Why the Great Wall of China Is INVISIBLE from Space (Space Myths, Busted)

Researched 2026-10-15 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview, gemini-3.5-flash-lite) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: For over two centuries, a popular legend claimed the Great Wall of China is the only human structure visible from the Moon. The rumor began in a 1754 letter by antiquarian William Stukeley, centuries before spaceflight existed. But when humans actually reached space, physics destroyed the myth. From the Moon, spotting a ten-meter-wide wall is optically equivalent to spotting a human hair from a kilometer away. Even from the International Space Station, orbiting 400 kilometers up, the wall is roughly ten times narrower than the human eye's resolving limit of one arcminute. Compounding the problem, the wall was built from local stone and dirt, camouflaging it against the surrounding landscape. China's own first astronaut, Yang Liwei, confirmed he could not see it at all. While orbiting cameras with telephoto lenses have captured it, unaided human eyes simply cannot.

## Disputed or left out

- Origin of the Moon visibility claim: Source S1 and S4 cite William Stukeley's 1754 letter, whereas S5 attributes the start of the myth to a 1923 National Geographic article, and S4 also highlights Richard Halliburton's 1938 book. A script should attribute: 'According to historical records, the myth dates back to at least a 1754 letter by William Stukeley.'
- Human remains in the wall: S1 and S6 state that no human bones or bodies were ever incorporated into the mortar or structure, whereas S5 claims deceased workers were buried in its foundation. A script should note: 'Urban legends claim bodies were built into the wall, but archaeological evidence finds no human bones in the mortar.'
- Naked-eye orbital visibility: S1 mentions past astronauts Gene Cernan and Ed Lu claimed to see it under exceptional conditions, but S2, S3, and S4 demonstrate that human eye resolution limits and camouflage make naked-eye detection virtually impossible without magnification. A script should state: 'While a couple of astronauts claimed brief glimpses, optical physics and NASA confirm it cannot be reliably resolved by unaided human vision.'
