# ep086 research: The Star That Survived Its Own Supernova (Space Got Weird)

Researched 2026-10-08 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview, gemini-3.5-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: In 1181, astronomers witnessed a rare cosmic blast: supernova SN 1181. Normally, a supernova obliterates the star. But in this case, something survived. At the center of the remnant IRAS 00500+6713 lies WD J005311, a zombie white dwarf. This star is the surviving product of a double degenerate merger where a carbon-oxygen white dwarf crashed into an oxygen-neon white dwarf. This collision triggered a sub-luminous Type Iax supernova. X-ray data from the XMM-Newton telescope revealed that the surviving central star is currently blowing an intense stellar wind enriched with carbon-burning ashes. This fast stellar wind crashes directly into the slow supernova ejecta, creating a highly complex and chaotic interaction zone. This unique stellar survivor shows that some stars can live through their own explosions.

## Disputed or left out

- The historical association of SN 1181 with the supernova remnant 3C 58 and its pulsar is now disproved because 3C 58 is much older (3500 to 5000+ years old); the script should leave this out to avoid confusion.
- The central star is occasionally designated as WS35 in optical studies; the script should stick to its primary designation WD J005311.
- A recent study estimates the exact explosion year as 1152 plus or minus some decades, but since this is consistent with 1181, the script should stick to the historically recorded date of 1181.
