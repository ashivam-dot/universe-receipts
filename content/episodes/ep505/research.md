# ep505 research: Vampire Stars Reverse Aging in This Collapsed Cluster (Space Got Weird)

Researched 2026-11-25 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.5-flash, gemini-3.6-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: In 1764, comet hunter Charles Messier spotted a faint smudge in the constellation Capricornus and logged it as a starless nebula. He was looking at Messier 30, a massive, 13-billion-year-old globular cluster containing hundreds of thousands of stars. But deep inside, Messier 30 holds a chaotic secret. Its inner core underwent a gravitational meltdown known as core collapse. Densely packing half its mass into a sphere just 17 light-years across, stars began crowding together at extreme densities. In this stellar traffic jam, old stars collided or entered tight binary loops. Some acted like cosmic vampires, leeching mass and hydrogen fuel from their neighbors. This sudden influx of fresh hydrogen rejuvenated the aging suns, creating blue stragglers—vampire stars that defy cosmic aging by burning bright, hot, and blue long after they should have died. Hubble images now reveal this stellar fountain of youth hidden in deep space.
