# ep406 research: Jupiter Will Outlive the Death of Our Sun (The Universe Is Terrifying)

Researched 2026-11-11 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview, gemini-3.5-flash, gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.6-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: Long before the Sun dies, Earth will face dramatic transformations. Driven by mantle convection, tectonic plates moving a few centimeters per year will merge the continents into a new supercontinent in a repeating cycle every few hundred million years. In about 50 million years, Africa will smash into Europe, closing the Mediterranean Sea and raising massive mountains. Geologists predict future supercontinents like Amasia or Pangaea Proxima, where extreme climates and violent monsoons dominate. But Earth's ultimate fate rests with the Sun. In about five billion years, our star will exhaust its core hydrogen fuel and balloon into a red giant. While inner worlds like Mercury and Venus are swallowed, gas giants Jupiter and Saturn could survive, continuing to orbit the dense white dwarf remnant left behind after the Sun's outer layers blow away.

## Disputed or left out

- The exact duration of the supercontinent cycle varies across sources (400-500 million years versus 500-700 million years); scripts should refer broadly to 'every few hundred million years'.
- The final arrangement of the next supercontinent is disputed between models like Amasia and Pangaea Proxima; scripts should frame these as competing geological predictions.
- Whether Earth itself will be completely engulfed during the Sun's red giant phase is uncertain; scripts should note Earth's survival as an open question.
