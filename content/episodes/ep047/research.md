# ep047 research: The Myth of the Moon's Dark Side (Space Myths, Busted)

Researched 2026-10-04 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.6-flash, gemini-3.8-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: Despite pop culture myths, there is no permanent dark side of the Moon. Because the Moon is tidally locked to Earth, one hemisphere always faces away from our planet, but it receives just as much sunlight as the near side. Both halves cycle through day and night over a roughly 29.5-day period. When Earth sees a full Moon, the far side is cast in complete darkness; during a new Moon, the far side is bathed in full sunlight. The word 'dark' historically meant 'unseen' because human eyes had never beheld it until the space age. In October 1959, the Soviet Luna 3 probe beamed back the first grainy photographs, and Apollo astronauts later observed it in person. Because the Moon blocks direct radio communication with Earth, exploration requires relay satellites. Today, robotic missions like China's Chang'e 4 and Chang'e 6 have successfully landed on and sampled this rugged, cratered hemisphere.

## Disputed or left out

- Who 'first saw' the far side: S6 poetically credits the Soviet engineers who received Luna 3's radioed images in 1959, while S1 credits Apollo 8 astronauts with seeing it directly with human eyes in 1968. A script should clearly distinguish between seeing photographs and direct in-person viewing.
- Crater density difference: S1 mentions competing scientific theories for why the near side has fewer visible craters (lava resurfacing obscuring craters vs. Earth's primordial heat creating a thicker far-side crust vs. a slow-impact companion moon). A script should attribute these as competing theories rather than settled fact.
