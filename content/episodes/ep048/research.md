# ep048 research: The Colossal Cosmic Drain Pulling Our Galaxy (Scale Breaker)

Researched 2026-10-04 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.5-flash, gemini-3.5-flash-lite) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: For generations, we thought our cosmic neighborhood ended at the edge of the Milky Way, or perhaps our local group of galaxies. But in September 2014, astronomers mapping the heavens made a mind-bending discovery. Our home galaxy is just a tiny speck inside a newly mapped, colossal supercluster named Laniakea—Hawaiian for 'immense heaven.' Spanning up to 500 million light-years and packed with the mass of 100 quadrillion Suns, this cosmic web binds 100,000 galaxies together. To map it, scientists didn't just look at where galaxies are; they tracked how they move. They discovered that all 100,000 galaxies are sliding along gravitational pathways, like water flowing down a valley, toward a mysterious focal point called the Great Attractor. Yet, this titanic structure is not permanent. Dark energy is actively pulling Laniakea apart, meaning our 'immense heaven' will eventually dissolve into the void.

## Disputed or left out

- The physical size of Laniakea: S1 cites roughly 400 million light-years, while S3, S4, and S7 cite 500 million light-years. A script should use '400 to 500 million light-years' to remain accurate.
- The definition and permanence of Laniakea: While originally defined as a supercluster, subsequent studies suggest it is not gravitationally bound and will be torn apart by dark energy, prompting some astronomers to call it a 'supercluster cocoon' or 'basin of attraction'. A script should mention that Laniakea is projected to dissolve in the far future.
