# ep263 research: The Black Hole Vacuum Myth (Space Myths, Busted)

Researched 2026-10-31 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.6-flash, gemini-3.7-flash, gemini-3.8-flash, gemini-3.8-flash-tts) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: Contrary to popular belief, black holes do not act like cosmic vacuum cleaners that suck in everything around them. Far away, a black hole's gravitational pull is no different from any normal celestial object of the exact same mass. If the Sun were replaced by a black hole of equal mass, Earth wouldn't get sucked in; planets would simply continue orbiting as usual, though space would turn dark and cold. All black holes spin, with some rotating over 1,000 times per second. They form when massive stars collapse in supernovae, leaving behind event horizons where not even light can escape. At the center of our Milky Way sits Sagittarius A*, a supermassive black hole four million times the mass of the Sun, proven by tracking the orbits of surrounding stars. When stars like our Sun eventually die in five billion years, they become white dwarfs, not black holes.

## Disputed or left out

- Mass estimate for Sagittarius A*: S1 specifies about 4.3 million solar masses while S3 rounds to 4 million times the Sun's mass. The script should use 'about 4 million solar masses'.
- Coining of the phrase 'black hole': S1 notes conflicting accounts regarding John Wheeler adopting the term at a 1967 lecture versus earlier print uses by Marcia Bartusiak, Ann Ewing, and Robert Dicke. The script should omit origin stories of the term.
- Earth's survival during the Sun's red giant phase: S2 cites conflicting studies from 2008 (vaporization) and 2026 (survival). The script should leave out Earth's ultimate fate or present it as uncertain.
