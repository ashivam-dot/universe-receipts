# ep236 research: The Spy Mirror Hunting for Alien Worlds (Scale Breaker)

Researched 2026-10-25 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.6-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: When NASA set out to map the dark cosmos, astronomers envisioned a modest infrared survey telescope. But in 2012, America's spy satellite agency, the National Reconnaissance Office, surprised NASA by handing over two classified 2.4-meter mirrors built for orbital surveillance. NASA adapted one of those reconnaissance optics into its next flagship observatory: the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope, named after the agency's pioneering first chief of astronomy. Launched aboard a Falcon Heavy rocket in August 2026 to the Sun-Earth L2 point, Roman delivers the razor-sharp image resolution of the Hubble Space Telescope across a field of view 100 times wider. Over its first five years of science operations, Roman will scan more sky than Hubble imaged in over three decades. Armed with a 300-megapixel camera and a starlight-blocking coronagraph, it turns former spy hardware outward to trace dark energy and uncover thousands of hidden exoplanets.

## Disputed or left out

- Field of view multiplier relative to Hubble: S1, S2, and S5 describe Roman's field of view as 100 times larger than Hubble's imaging cameras, while S4 cites 200 times Hubble's infrared view. The script should explicitly clarify it is 100 times larger than Hubble's standard imaging camera or up to 200 times its infrared view.
- Prime mission duration: S1, S3, S4, and S6 specify a 5-year prime mission, whereas early reporting in S7 noted a prime mission of at least six years. The script should stick to the baselined five-year primary mission.
- Cost effect of the donated NRO mirror: S5 reported that using the surplus mirror could be enlarged at no extra cost, while S1 stated the mirror's boundary conditions risked pushing total mission costs over a custom clean-sheet design. Scripts should avoid claiming the mirror made the total mission cheaper.
