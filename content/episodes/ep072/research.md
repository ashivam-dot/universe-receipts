# ep072 research: The Solar Storm That Missed Earth (Near Misses)

Researched 2026-10-07 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.7-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: On July 23, 2012, the Sun launched an ultra-fast solar superstorm that tore through Earth's orbit. It was a Carrington-class coronal mass ejection, packing the power of a billion hydrogen bombs and traveling four times faster than normal. Had it hit, the consequences would have been catastrophic, inflicting over two trillion dollars in damage and knocking out power grids and water supplies globally. Experts estimate recovery would have taken up to ten years. Yet, the public barely noticed. Why? Because the storm missed Earth by a margin of just nine days. Instead of wiping out our modern power grid, the blast hit NASA’s STEREO-A spacecraft, which was safely positioned outside Earth's magnetosphere to record the event. The storm's path had been cleared by a smaller solar eruption four days earlier, allowing it to maintain its terrifying speed. Scientists warn there is a twelve percent chance a similar Carrington-class storm will strike Earth in any given decade.

## Disputed or left out

- The exact margin of the near-miss: S1, S6, and S7 state the storm missed Earth by 'roughly nine days', while S2, S4, and S5 state 'one week' or 'a week'. The script should resolve this by stating 'about a week to nine days' or 'nine days'.
- The peak speed of the CME: S1 notes a radial speed of 3,300 km/s and an average speed of 2,000 km/s; S3 and S6 cite a peak speed of more than 2,000 km/s; S5 cites 3,000 km/s, and S7 cites 2,500-3,000 km/s. The script should generalise this by saying 'over 2,000 kilometers per second' or 'four times faster than normal'.
- Where the 1859 aurora was visible: S2 and S5 mention Cuba, while S3 and S6 mention Hawaii. The script can attribute this widely by saying 'seen in tropical latitudes like Cuba and Hawaii'.
