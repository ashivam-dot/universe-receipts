# ep378 research: The Planet That Spins Backward (Space Myths, Busted)

Researched 2026-11-07 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview, gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.6-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: Venus is one of the most bizarre worlds in our solar system. Unlike almost every other planet, Venus spins backward in a retrograde clockwise direction. If you could stand on its scorched surface, the Sun would rise in the west and set in the east. Even stranger, a single rotation takes about 243 Earth days—meaning a day on Venus is actually longer than its 224.7-day orbital year! But while the planet's solid body barely turns, its toxic atmosphere of 96.5% carbon dioxide super-rotates, whipping around the world in just four Earth days. Under a crushing surface pressure 92 times that of Earth and temperatures reaching 464 °C, Venus presented a bizarre mystery to early space probes like Pioneer Venus and Venus Express. From mysterious dark cloud absorbers affecting its weather to controversial hints of atmospheric phosphine, Venus continues to defy planetary norms.

## Disputed or left out

- Mechanism of Venusian spin reversal: Scientific American notes scientists dispute whether Venus flipped its axis 180 degrees due to solar tidal forces or slowed to a stop and reversed direction (attribute: 'according to theoretical models').
- Detection and concentration of phosphine gas: Sources dispute the exact abundance of phosphine, with initial reports claiming ~20 ppb while re-analyses found data processing errors lowering it to 1 ppb or zero (attribute: 'according to initial telescopic observations, which remain contested').
- Origin of upper atmosphere sulfur dioxide spikes: ESA and Wikipedia sources disagree on whether spikes are caused by active volcanic eruptions or atmospheric circulation cycles (attribute: 'researchers attribute the changes to either active volcanism or atmospheric circulation').
- Presence of atmospheric lightning: Wikipedia sources note VLF and whistler mode detections suggesting lightning, while Akatsuki and Parker Solar Probe data show low or non-planetary signals (attribute: 'planetary scientists continue to debate whether lightning occurs').
