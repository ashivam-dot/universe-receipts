# ep483 research: What Happens When Betelgeuse Explodes? (The Universe Is Terrifying)

Researched 2026-11-22 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.5-flash, gemini-3.6-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: In late 2019, one of the most recognizable stars in the night sky began to fade rapidly, fueling worldwide speculation that a cataclysmic stellar explosion was imminent. Betelgeuse, the fiery red shoulder of Orion, plunged to its faintest levels in a century, dropping from the top ten brightest stars down past the top twenty. Betelgeuse is a cosmic titan: wielded at roughly twenty times the Sun's mass, it is bloated enough that if placed in our solar system, it would engulf Mercury, Venus, Earth, and Mars. A runaway star hurtling through space at thirty kilometers per second, it is doomed to end in a brilliant Type II supernova within 100,000 years. When it detonates, it will burn bright enough to be seen in broad daylight and cast nighttime shadows on Earth for months without harming our planet. Ultimately, telescopes proved the 2019 scare wasn't the final blast, but a titanic ejection of gas that cooled into a temporary dust veil.

## Disputed or left out

- Distance to Betelgeuse: Estimates in the sources range from roughly 400 to over 700 light-years (commonly cited around 500–640 ly). The script should state 'roughly 500 to 600 light-years away.'
- Bow shock width: Wikipedia and NASA state roughly 4 light-years across, while ESA's Akari team measured it at about 3 light-years. The script should say 'three to four light-years wide.'
- Cause of the Great Dimming: Hubble ultraviolet data pointed to a surface mass ejection forming an obscuring dust cloud, while some submillimetre studies suggested giant cool starspots on the photosphere without dust. The script should attribute the dust cloud mechanism to Hubble and ESO observations.
- Supernova timeline: Most sources state within the next 100,000 years, while a couple mention up to a million years. The script should state 'expected within the next 100,000 years, according to astronomers.'
