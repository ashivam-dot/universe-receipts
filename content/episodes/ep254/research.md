# ep254 research: A Star ESCAPING the Milky Way (Scale Breaker)

Researched 2026-10-29 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.6-flash, gemini-3.8-flash-tts) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: Everyday citizens just made a discovery that breaks the scales of galactic speed. Using NASA’s WISE mission data, volunteers with the Backyard Worlds project spotted a faint object named CWISE J1249+3621. It is an L subdwarf—a rare, low-mass object sitting right on the line between a star and a brown dwarf. But while most stars orbit the Milky Way at a steady pace, this one is screaming through space at over one million miles per hour. That is fast enough to completely break free from the Milky Way’s gravity and vanish into intergalactic space. Located just 400 light-years away, it is the closest hypervelocity star ever found. Astronomers believe it was either 'kicked' by a massive supernova or catapulted by a pair of black holes in a star cluster. This ancient relic, likely from the galaxy’s first generation of stars, is on a one-way trip out of our neighborhood forever.
