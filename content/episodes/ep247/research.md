# ep247 research: The Space Telescope That Hunts on Machine Instinct (Mission Files)

Researched 2026-10-28 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.6-flash, gemini-3.7-flash, gemini-3.8-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: Most space telescopes are lumbering giants pointed cautiously by human controllers days in advance. But when a dying star collapses into a black hole billions of light-years away, the initial gamma-ray flash lasts mere seconds. Enter the Neil Gehrels Swift Observatory. Launched by NASA in 2004, Swift was not named as an acronym, but after the nimble bird that pivots mid-flight. Using a coded aperture mask embedded with 52,000 lead tiles, Swift continuously scans wide swathes of the sky. The moment it detects a burst, its onboard computers take total control, instantly slewing the entire multi-ton spacecraft in under a minute without waiting for commands from Earth. By aiming its onboard X-ray and ultraviolet telescopes at the coordinates before the explosion fades, Swift caught cosmic events over 13 billion light-years distant. Originally built for a two-year mission, this autonomous robot guarded the high-energy skies for two decades.

## Disputed or left out

- Initial orbital parameters: S1 states Swift was inserted into an orbit of 585 by 604 km at 20.60 degrees inclination, while S6 lists a 600 km circular orbit at 22 degrees inclination. Scripts should describe the orbit generally as a low Earth orbit around 600 kilometers.
- LINK rescue timeline and mission outcome: S5 reports the rescue mission launching on a Friday in mid-2025, whereas S1 documents a launch on 3 July 2026 and subsequent failure to rendezvous on 19 August 2026. A short script should either omit future mission dates or attribute the failed rescue attempt directly to NASA and Katalyst reports.
- Burst Alert Telescope detector tile count: S1 cites both 32,768 and 32,500 cadmium zinc telluride detector units in different sections, while S6 notes 256 modules of 128 elements (equaling 32,768). Scripts should refer to over 32,000 detector elements or focus on the agreed 52,000 mask tiles.
