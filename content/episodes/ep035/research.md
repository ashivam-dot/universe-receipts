# ep035 research: The Asteroid Passing Closer Than Our Satellites (Near Misses)

Researched 2026-10-03 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.7-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: In December 2004, astronomers calculating the path of newly discovered asteroid 99942 Apophis announced an alarming forecast: a 2.7 percent chance of slamming into Earth on Friday, April 13, 2029. It became the first object in history to reach Level 4 on the Torino impact hazard scale. Subsequent observations and archival precovery images eliminated the 2029 collision risk, though lingering concerns over potential impacts in 2036 and 2068 persisted for years. Finally, March 2021 radar observations definitively ruled out any Earth collision for at least a century. Yet the 2029 encounter remains breathtaking. Apophis, a 450-meter space rock, will sweep just 31,600 kilometers above Earth's surface—closer than geostationary satellites. Visible to billions with the naked eye, its orbit will be fundamentally warped from an Aten-class into an Apollo-class trajectory as Earth's tidal forces stretch its surface, setting the stage for rendezvous missions by NASA's OSIRIS-APEX and ESA's Ramses.

## Disputed or left out

- Impact energy estimates vary across sources: S1 cites 1,200 megatons of TNT, S4 notes more than 1,000 megatons, while S7 reports an older NASA estimate of 400 megatons. The script should attribute modern assessments as 'more than 1,000 megatons of TNT'.
- Naming connection to the television series Stargate SG-1: S1 notes co-discoverers were fans of the show's villain Apophis, but co-discoverer David Tholen explicitly denied naming the asteroid after the fictional character, calling the link coincidental. The script should attribute the name strictly to the Egyptian mythological deity.
- Exact minimum distance of the 2029 flyby: estimates in the sources range from 29,470 km (S2) to 30,600 km (S4), 31,600 km (S1), and roughly 32,000 km (S3). The script should describe it as 'around 31,600 kilometers' or 'less than 32,000 kilometers' above Earth's surface.
