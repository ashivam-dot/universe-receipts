# ep111 research: The Megastructure That Breaks Physics (Scale Breaker)

Researched 2026-10-09 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview, gemini-3.5-flash, gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.7-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: In 2021, student Alexia Lopez at the University of Central Lancashire made a mind-bending discovery while analyzing data from the Sloan Digital Sky Survey. By using distant, ultra-bright background quasars as giant cosmic lamps to illuminate intervening gas, she uncovered the Giant Arc—an almost symmetrical crescent of galaxies spanning a staggering 3.3 billion light-years. Located over 9 billion light-years away in the constellation Boötes, this megastructure stretches across roughly 1/15th of the radius of the observable universe. According to standard cosmology, structures shouldn't exceed 1.2 billion light-years because gravity hasn't had enough time to form anything larger since the Big Bang. The Giant Arc directly challenges the Cosmological Principle, which assumes the universe is uniform on its grandest scales. Along with Lopez's other discovery, the 1.3-billion-light-year-wide Big Ring, this gargantuan structure suggests our standard model of the universe might be incomplete or entirely wrong.

## Disputed or left out

- The exact distance to the Giant Arc is reported as 9.2 billion light-years in S1 and S4, but 9.3 billion light-years in S2; the script should use a general estimate of over 9 billion light-years.
- The visual scale of the Arc if seen in the night sky is described as 20 full moons in S1 but 35 full moons in S2; the script should generally refer to dozens of full moons.
- Whether the Arc violates the cosmological principle is disputed by a 2025 study in S1, whereas S2, S3, and S4 treat it as a direct challenge; the script should frame this as an active scientific debate.
