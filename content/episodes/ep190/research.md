# ep190 research: How NASA Trapped Speeding Comet Dust (Mission Files)

Researched 2026-10-18 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview, gemini-3.5-flash, gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.6-flash, gemini-3.8-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: In 1999, NASA launched Stardust on a seven-year, nearly three-billion-mile journey to capture dust from a comet. Because particles colliding at thousands of miles per hour would vaporize on contact with ordinary surfaces, engineers deployed a tennis-racket-shaped collector packed with ultra-low-density aerogel—a porous substance that is 99.8 percent empty space. In January 2004, Stardust flew past Comet Wild 2, gently braking microscopic cometary grains and passing interstellar dust without destroying them. On January 15, 2006, its sample return capsule detached and parachuted safely into the Utah desert, completing the first mission ever to return comet samples to Earth. While the mothership was redirected to photograph Comet Tempel 1, tens of thousands of online citizen scientists searched digital microscope images of the aerogel, successfully uncovering organic molecules and seven rare grains of interstellar dust older than our Solar System.

## Disputed or left out

- Closest approach distance to Comet Wild 2: S1 states 237 km, S5 states 236 km, and S2 states 250 km (155 miles). Scripts should state approximately 240 to 250 kilometers.
- Capsule touchdown time: S1 and S5 report touchdown at 10:12 UT/GMT, while S2 reports 10:10 UT. Scripts should state around 10:10 to 10:12 UTC.
- First mission to return extraterrestrial samples beyond lunar orbit: S4 notes Stardust was the first NASA sample return mission since Apollo, while S2 clarifies it was the second designed to return samples from beyond lunar orbit, following Genesis. Scripts should focus specifically on Stardust being the first to return samples from a comet.
- Announcement timing of the seven interstellar dust grains: S1 mentions both April and August 2014, S4 specifies April 2014, and S2 specifies August 2014. Scripts should attribute the announcement generally to 2014.
