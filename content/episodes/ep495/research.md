# ep495 research: The Space Rock That Struck a Woman and Sparked a Lawsuit (Space Got Weird)

Researched 2026-11-23 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview, gemini-3.5-flash, gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.6-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: On November 30, 1954, 34-year-old Ann Hodges was napping on her couch in Sylacauga, Alabama, when an 8.5-pound meteorite crashed through her roof, ricocheted off a radio cabinet, and struck her side, leaving a massive bruise. Hodges became the first documented human in history confirmed to be struck by a meteorite. Following the incident, the U.S. Air Force confiscated the rock before returning it. Her landlady then sued for ownership, claiming it had fallen on her property. The Hodgeses settled by paying $500 to keep the space rock, but by then, public interest had cooled, and they could not find a buyer willing to pay big money. Overwhelmed by media attention and severe emotional distress, Ann eventually donated the rock to the Alabama Museum of Natural History. Meanwhile, a local farmer found a smaller fragment of the same meteorite and successfully sold it to the Smithsonian to buy a house and car.

## Disputed or left out

- Exact time of impact: Ann Hodges remembered the hit occurring around 2:00 PM local time, but official records list the meteor entry time at 12:46 PM CST. Attribute or specify official report time.
- Ann's age at impact: S4 lists her age as 31, while S2, S5, and S7 state 34. Attribute or omit exact age.
- Landlord name and gender: S1 and S7 spell the name Bertie Guy, S3 spells it Birdie Guy, while S6 incorrectly uses male pronouns for the landlord. Attribute as landlord Mrs. Guy.
- Weight of Hodges fragment: S1, S4, S5, and S7 state 8.5 pounds, while S2 lists 9 pounds. Script should use 'about 8.5 pounds'.
