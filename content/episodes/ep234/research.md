# ep234 research: The Soviet Lander That Whispered From Venus (Mission Files)

Researched 2026-10-24 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: In 1970, the Soviet Union sent Venera 7 to the most hostile environment in the solar system: the surface of Venus. Built with a heavy titanium hull to withstand 180 atmospheres of pressure, the probe was a literal tank. However, during its final descent, the parachute failed, sending the lander into a 60 km/h freefall. It slammed into the Venusian soil and appeared to go silent. For weeks, scientists thought the surface mission was a failure. But radio astronomer Oleg Rzhiga refused to give up, painstakingly combing through the recording tapes. He discovered a miracle: a signal 100 times weaker than normal, buried in the background noise. The probe had survived the crash and landed on its side, pointing its antenna away from Earth. For 23 minutes, it whispered the first-ever data from the surface of another planet, confirming Venus was a 475-degree-Celsius furnace.
