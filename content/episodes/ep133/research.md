# ep133 research: Nature's 20,000-Mile GEOMETRY on Saturn (Space Got Weird)

Researched 2026-10-12 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.8-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: In 1981, NASA's Voyager probes snapped tantalizing views of Saturn's north pole, revealing something that looked impossible: a giant, nearly perfect hexagon carved into the clouds. Measuring over 20,000 miles wide—large enough to swallow two Earths—this six-sided jet stream whips eastward at over 200 miles per hour, surrounding a violent central polar hurricane. When the Cassini orbiter arrived decades later, it confirmed the geometric vortex was still spinning, locked tightly with Saturn's internal rotation period of 10 hours and 39 minutes. Over its multi-year mission, Cassini even watched the hexagon shift in color from deep blue to golden haze as solar radiation reacted with atmospheric methane during the long Saturnian summer. Laboratory physicists eventually cracked the geometry: spinning fluid in circular tanks naturally buckles into stable polygons when sharp speed gradients clash, proving that fluid physics can sculpt pure geometry out of atmospheric chaos.

## Disputed or left out

- Side length measurements: S1 cites approximately 14,500 km (9,000 miles), while S5 and S7 cite approximately 13,800 km (8,600 miles). Scripts should state 'around 14,000 kilometers' or 'longer than Earth's diameter'.
- Jet wind speeds: S1, S4, and S7 state wind speeds around 200 mph (320 km/h), whereas S2 states about 300 mph (500 km/h). Scripts should qualify perimeter wind speeds as exceeding 200 to 300 mph.
- Discovery date of the pattern: S1 gives 1987 as Godfrey's discovery year, while S6 and S7 cite his published paper in 1988. Attribute simply as 'discovered in the late 1980s from Voyager images'.
- Depth of the mechanism: Sources dispute whether the hexagon is a shallow atmospheric wave (S4, S6) or a deep-rooted convective jet extending thousands of kilometers into the interior (S2, S6). Frame this as an ongoing scientific debate.
