# ep491 research: Why Astronauts See 16 Sunrises Every Day (Scale Breaker)

Researched 2026-11-23 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.6-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: Assembled in low Earth orbit starting with the launch of the Zarya module in 1998, the International Space Station is the largest human-made spacecraft ever built. Spanning the length and width of an American football field with an internal volume comparable to a Boeing 747 airliner, this massive international laboratory orbits Earth at roughly 17,500 miles per hour at an altitude of 250 miles. Because it completes a full orbit around the planet approximately every 90 minutes along a 51.6-degree orbital path, crew members aboard experience 15 to 16 sunrises and sunsets every 24 hours. Continuously inhabited since November 2000 through a partnership between NASA, Roscosmos, ESA, JAXA, and CSA, the ISS serves as a primary hub for zero-gravity research. However, due to structural aging and air leaks, space agencies plan to deorbit the station around 2030, guiding its destruction in Earth's atmosphere.

## Disputed or left out

- Total mass of the station varies slightly across sources (S5 lists 430 metric tons, S6 lists 925,000 pounds / 420,000 kg, and S1 quotes 500 tons); the script should state 'over 400 metric tons' or avoid precise mass figures.
- Orbital period is stated as 93 minutes in S1 and 92 minutes in S6; the script should attribute or round to 'roughly 90 minutes'.
