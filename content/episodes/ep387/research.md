# ep387 research: Rosetta's 2 MPH Comet Landing (Mission Files)

Researched 2026-11-08 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.6-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: In March 2004, the European Space Agency launched Rosetta on an audacious 1.3-billion-euro expedition to chase down Comet 67P/Churyumov–Gerasimenko. After a decade-long trek through deep space powered by four planetary gravity assists at Earth and Mars, Rosetta made history in August 2014 by becoming the first spacecraft ever to enter orbit around a comet nucleus. Arriving at the bizarre, rubber-duck-shaped cosmic wanderer, Rosetta mapped cliffs and active gas vents before deploying its companion lander, Philae. Although Philae bounced twice and went silent within days, Rosetta escorted the comet around the Sun, discovering molecular oxygen in its coma. But as the comet drifted outbound toward Jupiter, dwindling solar light starved the probe of power. Instead of letting Rosetta freeze in darkness, scientists commanded a slow-motion dive, ending the twelve-year voyage on September 30, 2016, by touching down on the comet at a gentle walking pace.

## Disputed or left out

- Exact duration of Philae battery life: S1 states 'two days later' in one section and 'after three days' in another; S4 states 'after three days'; S6 notes 'surviving just a few days'. Scripts should say 'within a few days' or 'two to three days'.
- Origin of terrestrial water: S1 notes that early Rosetta data showing a deuterium-to-hydrogen ratio three times that of Earth was updated by a 2024 reanalysis finding Earth-like ratios. A script should avoid claiming definitively whether comet 67P matches Earth's oceans without qualifying the evolving analysis.
