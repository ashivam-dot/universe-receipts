# ep116 research: The Scrap-Metal Probe That Conquered Venus (Mission Files)

Researched 2026-10-10 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: To map Venus, NASA built a 'Frankenstein' spacecraft. Magellan was pieced together from leftover spare parts of the Voyager, Galileo, and Mariner 9 missions, including a recycled Voyager antenna. Launched in 1989 aboard Space Shuttle Atlantis, it became the first deep space probe deployed from a shuttle. Upon arriving at Venus in 1990, Magellan faced a world of crushing atmospheric pressure and temperatures reaching 475 degrees Celsius. Because Venus is perpetually cloaked in thick clouds, Magellan used synthetic aperture radar to pierce the haze. Over several 243-day cycles, it mapped an incredible 98% of the planet's surface, revealing a globally volcanic wasteland with no Earth-like plate tectonics and almost zero water erosion. In 1993, Magellan pioneered aerobraking to circularize its orbit. Finally, in October 1994, engineers commanded the probe to make a dramatic, intentional plunge into the Venusian atmosphere, gathering aerodynamic data before being vaporized.

## Disputed or left out

- The exact date of communication loss and destruction: S1 and S2 record that final contact was lost on October 13, 1994, while S5 and S6 date the signal loss to October 12, 1994, with the final loss of the spacecraft occurring on October 13. The script should attribute this timeline carefully or generally state 'mid-October 1994'.
- Radar imaging resolution: Wording varies on the design target versus the actual processed resolution, ranging from 120-300 meters (S2) to 75 meters per pixel (S3, S5). The script should state the final resolution was roughly 75 to 100 meters.
