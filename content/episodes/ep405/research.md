# ep405 research: The Spacecraft One Light-Day Away From Earth (Scale Breaker)

Researched 2026-11-12 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.8-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: In September 1977, NASA launched Voyager 1 from Cape Canaveral aboard a Titan rocket, embarking on a grand tour of the outer planets. Built and operated by the Jet Propulsion Laboratory, the spacecraft made groundbreaking flybys of Jupiter and Saturn, discovering active volcanoes on the moon Io and examining Saturn's giant moon Titan. Powered by plutonium thermoelectric generators, the probe continued driving outward past the planets. In August 2012, Voyager 1 crossed into interstellar space, officially becoming the most distant human-made object in history. To keep its high-gain antenna locked onto Earth via the Deep Space Network, engineers successfully fired backup thrusters that had slept unused for decades. Led by project scientist Ed Stone, the craft carries a 12-inch Golden Record compiled by Carl Sagan and Timothy Ferris, designed to carry the sounds and images of Earth through the galaxy forever.

## Disputed or left out

- The exact timing and location of Voyager 1's crossing of the termination shock was debated among scientists (some claiming late 2003 and others late 2004) because its primary solar-wind detector stopped functioning in 1990; script should attribute or frame as inferred from indirect particle data.
- Whether crossing the heliopause means leaving the Solar System entirely is disputed semantically, as the Oort cloud extends far beyond; script should clarify it entered interstellar space without leaving the full gravitational boundary of the Oort cloud.
