# ep205 research: The Black Hole That Sings a B-flat (Scale Breaker)

Researched 2026-10-21 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview, gemini-3.5-flash, gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.7-flash, gemini-3.8-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: Space is widely known as a silent vacuum, but galaxy clusters break the rule. At the core of the Perseus Cluster, 250 million light-years away, a supermassive black hole pumps enormous energy into the surrounding multimillion-degree gas. In 2002, NASA's Chandra X-ray Observatory captured concentric ripples spreading across hundreds of thousands of light-years in the cluster's X-ray glow. Astronomers discovered these ripples were acoustic pressure waves generated by plasma bubbles blown by the black hole. The acoustic pitch was calculated to be a B-flat, exactly 57 octaves below middle C—the deepest cosmic note ever recorded. Its oscillation period is an astounding 9.6 million years, over a quadrillion times below the limits of human hearing. The tremendous energy carried by this cosmic hum prevents cluster gas from cooling and forming stars. Decades later, NASA sonified the waves by scaling their frequency up 144 quadrillion times, finally allowing humanity to hear the roar of a black hole.

## Disputed or left out

- The 9.6 million year wave period is explicitly noted in S1, whereas S5/S6 focus on the 57-octave figure and 2.5 billion year duration without quoting the 9.6 million year period; scripts should state the pitch as 57 octaves below middle C and attribute the multi-million-year period to Cambridge analysis.
- The exact discovery year is listed as 2002 for observations in S5 and S6, while formal announcement and papers date to 2003 (S1, S5); scripts can clarify observations were captured in 2002 and announced in 2003.
