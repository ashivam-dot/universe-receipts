# ep120 research: The Cosmic Giant Tearing Itself Apart (Scale Breaker)

Researched 2026-10-10 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.6-flash, gemini-3.7-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: Over seven billion light-years from Earth lies a cosmic colossus so massive that astronomers named it El Gordo, Spanish for 'The Fat One'. Discovered using telescopes in Chile and NASA's Chandra X-ray Observatory, this titan weighs as much as three quadrillion times the mass of our Sun. But El Gordo is not a single peaceful cluster. It is a catastrophic collision between two separate galaxy subclusters smashing into each other at millions of miles per hour. The violent impact wretches normal matter apart, slowing down superheated X-ray gas while unseen dark matter sails straight through unhindered. This sheer concentration of matter warps spacetime itself into a cosmic magnifying glass, bending the light of background galaxies behind it. Representing the most massive and X-ray bright cluster ever found at such an immense distance, El Gordo pushes the very limits of our cosmic models.

## Disputed or left out

- Distance estimates vary among sources: S1, S3, and S5 state approximately 7 billion light-years, S6 and S7 cite 7.3 billion light-years, while S2 lists 9.7 billion light-years; the script should attribute the distance as roughly 7 billion light-years.
- Total mass estimates range from two million billion times the mass of the Sun (S5) to three million billion / three quadrillion solar masses (S4, S6, S7), with S1 noting an initial estimate slightly below three quadrillion later refined to 2.1 quadrillion solar masses; scripts should state the mass spans two to three quadrillion solar masses.
- Cosmological model consistency: S3 reports the cluster is consistent with standard dark matter and Big Bang models, while S1 documents scientific arguments that its high collision speed and huge mass challenge the standard Lambda-CDM model; the script should present this as an ongoing scientific debate.
