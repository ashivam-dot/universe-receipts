# ep362 research: The Starquake That Hit Earth (The Universe Is Terrifying)

Researched 2026-11-06 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview, gemini-3.5-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: A dead star known as SGR 1900+14 is a magnetic monster lurking 20,000 light-years away. This 'magnetar' is a neutron star with a magnetic field trillions of times stronger than Earth’s. On August 27, 1998, it experienced a massive eruption so intense that it overwhelmed satellite sensors across our solar system. Even the shielded Rossi X-ray Timing Explorer was flooded with radiation. This blast was a giant flare—likely caused by a massive disruption of the star's crust—releasing a burst of energy that left the object emitting a pulsating tail as it spun every 5.16 seconds. Years later, NASA’s Spitzer Space Telescope discovered a mysterious 'ring of fire' seven light-years wide surrounding the star. This stationary ring is believed to be a permanent scar from the magnetar's violent past, proving that even the most distant stars can scream loud enough for the entire galaxy to hear.

## Disputed or left out

- The distance to SGR 1900+14 is disputed, with Wikipedia citing 20,000 light-years and NRAO citing 15,000 light-years; the script should use 'about 20,000 light-years' or mention the proximity to the galactic plane.
- The ionization of Earth's atmosphere is only detailed in Wikipedia, whereas scientific sources focus on satellite saturation; the script should focus on the impact on space-based sensors for better attribution.
- The origin of the Spitzer ring is labeled as 'unknown' in Wikipedia but as 'formed by a powerful flare' in the Newswise report; the script should attribute the flare theory to the Spitzer research team.
