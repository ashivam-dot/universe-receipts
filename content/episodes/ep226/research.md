# ep226 research: Why a dead star just SKIPPED a beat (The Universe Is Terrifying)

Researched 2026-10-23 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview, gemini-3.5-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: One thousand light-years away, a dead star collapsed into an ultra-dense sphere just 12 miles wide, spinning over eleven times per second. Born in a massive supernova over ten thousand years ago, the Vela Pulsar sweeps violent radiation jets across deep space like a cosmic lighthouse. But this dead star has a terrifying habit: every three years, it glitches. Without warning, the pulsar suddenly violently speeds up its spin. In 2016, astronomers monitoring the Mount Pleasant Observatory caught a glitch live for the very first time. Right as the star glitched, it skipped a beat entirely, missing a pulse before snapping back to speed in just five seconds. Scientists believe this violent hiccup happens when the pulsar's churning superfluid core suddenly grips its solid outer crust, transferring energy and revealing the extreme interior physics of one of the universe's deadliest engines.

## Disputed or left out

- Distance measurements vary slightly across sources: NASA and popular science sources state 1,000 light-years, Wikipedia states ~800 light-years, and HST parallax studies state ~294 parsecs (~960 light-years). Script should state 'roughly 1,000 light-years away'.
- Neutron star mass is cited as ~1.4 solar masses in S4 and ~1.5 solar masses in S5. Script should describe it as 'about 1.4 to 1.5 times the mass of our Sun'.
