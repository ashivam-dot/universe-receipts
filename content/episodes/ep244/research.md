# ep244 research: The Terrifying Myth of the Moon's Quicksand (Space Myths, Busted)

Researched 2026-10-25 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.8-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: Before Apollo 11, mission planners faced a terrifying question: was the lunar surface covered in a vast ocean of powdery dust that would swallow a 33,000-pound lander whole? To test the ground, NASA launched the robotic Surveyor probes directly at the Moon. In June 1966, Surveyor 1 touched down in Oceanus Procellarum, resting solidly on its three footpads without sinking. Later Surveyors scraped trenches with robotic scoops, confirming the regolith could easily support a crewed lander. But the Moon held a different surprise. The soil was not a quicksand trap; it was an abrasive layer of jagged rock and glass fragments. Worse, the airless environment made it electrostatically charged. When Surveyor 7 watched the lunar sunset, it photographed a strange twilight glow: tiny dust grains floating miles into the sky. When Apollo 12 astronauts visited Surveyor 3 three years later, they found it thoroughly coated in clinging, electrified grit.

## Disputed or left out

- Initial reports claimed bacteria inside the returned Surveyor 3 camera survived 31 months on the Moon, but subsequent studies attributed this to post-return laboratory contamination on Earth; scripts should note that the microbial survival claim was discredited as Earth contamination.
