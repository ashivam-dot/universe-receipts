# ep084 research: The Comet Landing That Bounced a Kilometer High (Mission Files)

Researched 2026-10-07 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.7-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: In November 2014, the European Space Agency's Rosetta mission made history when its lander, Philae, touched down on comet 67P/Churyumov-Gerasimenko after a ten-year journey. But the landing went wildly off-script. Philae's anchoring harpoons and gas thruster both failed to fire. Instead of staying put, the lander bounced off the surface, soaring nearly a kilometer high into the comet's ultra-low gravity. It drifted in space for almost two hours before touching down a second and then a third time. Philae finally wedged itself in a dark, rocky crevice, tilted at thirty degrees. Stuck in deep shadows, the lander received only ninety minutes of sunlight per day instead of the planned seven hours. Despite the desperate situation and a dying battery, Philae successfully transmitted scientific data from all ten of its instruments before slipping into hibernation.

## Disputed or left out

- The exact time of first touchdown varies slightly between 15:33 and 15:34 UTC; the script should use 'around 15:33 or 15:34 UTC'.
- The exact battery lifetime varies between 57 hours of actual operation and 60 to 64 hours of design limit; the script should say 'nearly 60 hours'.
- The initial reports of the landing mentioned only one or two touchdowns, but the consensus is three; the script should stick to three landings.
- The detailed smell of the comet is only mentioned in S6; the script must leave this claim out to satisfy the strict two-source constraint.
