# ep125 research: The Cold War Rocket Powered by Atomic Bombs (Mission Files)

Researched 2026-10-11 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.8-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: In 1958, American physicists Ted Taylor and Freeman Dyson led Project Orion at General Atomics, designing a massive interplanetary spacecraft propelled by atomic bombs. Conceived by Stanisław Ulam and funded by ARPA, the spacecraft operated by dropping nuclear explosives behind a giant steel pusher plate and detonating them at a rhythm of one bomb per second. While each blast would subject the plate to tens of thousands of g's, heavy shock absorbers were engineered to cushion the ride down to a gentle few g's for human passengers. To keep the steel pusher plate from eroding away under recurring nuclear fireballs, researchers discovered an elegant fix: spraying a thin layer of oil across the plate between explosions prevented ablation entirely. Despite achieving promising scale-model tests, the project was canceled in 1964 following the 1963 Partial Test Ban Treaty, which outlawed nuclear explosions in the atmosphere and outer space.

## Disputed or left out

- Official project shutdown year differs between sources (1964 in S1 and S3 versus 1965 in S2); script should attribute: 'canceled in 1964, with work winding down into 1965'.
- Pusher plate shock absorber and spalling mitigation designs varied between water-cooled springs, multi-stage gas pistons, and magnetic shielding; script should treat water-cooled springs as 'one early engineering solution investigated'.
- The claim that a nuclear containment test plate during Operation Plumbbob was launched into space at six times escape velocity is treated by physicists as a theoretical lower-bound calculation rather than confirmed fact, with calculations indicating it likely vaporized; script should attribute: 'according to a famous calculation'.
