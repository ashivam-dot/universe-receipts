# ep221 research: How the Suicide Squad Built NASA's Space Center (Mission Files)

Researched 2026-10-23 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.7-flash, gemini-3.8-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: In 1936, rocket science was considered reckless science fiction. At Caltech, graduate student Frank Malina teamed up with self-taught rocket enthusiasts Jack Parsons and Edward Forman to test dangerous homemade rocket motors. After a series of hazardous tests and explosions—including one that blasted an instrument gauge straight into a laboratory wall—the group was dubbed the 'Suicide Squad' and ordered off campus. Banished to the Arroyo Seco, a dry riverbed wash in Pasadena, the team continued their work using alcohol-fueled engines. Their successful experiments on Jet-Assisted Take-Off rockets soon attracted U.S. Army funding. By 1943, team members proposed naming their venture the Jet Propulsion Laboratory. After building Explorer 1, America's first satellite, in 1958, JPL was transferred to NASA. The ragtag band of campus outcasts ultimately laid the foundation for the agency that sent rovers across Mars and propelled Voyager into interstellar space.

## Disputed or left out

- Identity of the person who introduced lucky peanuts: S4 credits trajectory engineer Dick Wallace, while S1 notes it may have been either Wallace or project manager Harris Schurmeier. The script should attribute the act to 'a team member' or state 'according to one account, trajectory engineer Dick Wallace passed them out'.
- Campus size: S1 and S3 report JPL covers approximately 168 acres, whereas S4 reports a 177-acre site. The script should avoid citing an exact acreage figure.
