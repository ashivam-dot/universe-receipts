# ep099 research: The Martian Mountain Taller Than Mount Everest (Scale Breaker)

Researched 2026-10-09 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.5-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: At the center of Mars's 154-kilometer-wide Gale Crater sits Aeolis Mons, an enormous mountain known informally to NASA as Mount Sharp. Rising 5.5 kilometers (18,000 feet) above the crater floor, this towering peak rises higher than Mount Rainier stands above Seattle and possesses a greater base-to-peak rise than Mount Everest. Unlike volcanic peaks, Aeolis Mons is an immense stack of sedimentary rock, deposited billions of years ago when Gale Crater held lakes and streams for thousands to millions of years, before Martian winds eroded the surrounding basin into an isolated mountain. In August 2012, NASA landed Curiosity, a car-sized rover carrying ten scientific instruments, on the crater floor. Equipped with cameras and a rock drill, Curiosity has spent over a decade slowly crawling up the mountain's layered slopes, decoding how an ancient wet, potentially habitable Martian environment dried out into a barren desert.

## Disputed or left out

- Landing calendar date: North American time zones record the landing on the evening of August 5, 2012, whereas UTC and European time zones record August 6, 2012. A script should cite 'August 2012' or specify the time zone.
- Origin of lower sedimentary layers: Sources note an ongoing scientific debate over whether katabatic wind deposition or lakebed sedimentation played the dominant role in forming the lowest mound layers. A script should frame the lakebed origin as an evidence-supported interpretation.
- Source of organic molecules: Sources disagree on whether organic compounds detected by Curiosity originated from biological activity, abiotic water-rock chemistry, or interplanetary dust and meteorites. A script should describe them strictly as organic building blocks rather than proof of life.
