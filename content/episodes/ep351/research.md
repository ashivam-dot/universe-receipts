# ep351 research: The Blind Probe That Saved Itself (Mission Files)

Researched 2026-11-05 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.5-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: In 1998, NASA launched Deep Space 1, a $150 million gamble to prove robots could explore the solar system alone. It featured a futuristic ion engine and "Remote Agent," the first AI to fully control an interplanetary craft without human supervision. However, the experimental mission nearly died in 1999 when its star tracker—the "eye" it used to navigate—failed completely. Blind and drifting millions of miles from home, the probe was considered doomed. In a desperate move, engineers spent months rewriting the spacecraft's code to transform its science camera into a makeshift star tracker. The ingenious hack worked. Not only did the probe recover, but it went on to capture images of Comet Borrelly so detailed they surpassed those of the famous Giotto mission. After three years and 16,000 hours of engine thrust, Deep Space 1 remains in solar orbit today, a silent pioneer of autonomous spaceflight.

## Disputed or left out

- Flyby distance of asteroid Braille: S1, S2, and S3 state 26 km, but S5 states 15 km; the script should use 26 km as the primary figure.
- Flyby distance of Comet Borrelly: S2 states 2,171 km, while S3 and S5 use 2,200 km; the script should say 'about 2,200 kilometers.'
- Remote Agent control duration: S4 specifies the AI had control for three days, while S1 describes it more generally; the script should attribute the AI control to a 'multi-day experiment.'
