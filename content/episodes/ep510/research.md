# ep510 research: The Astronaut Who Threw a Solar Panel into Space (Mission Files)

Researched 2026-11-25 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.6-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: In December 1993, Space Shuttle Endeavour launched on STS-61, one of NASA's most complex shuttle missions, to repair the Hubble Space Telescope's blurry vision. Before launch, grit from a windstorm forced a pad switch, and a launch attempt was delayed by weather and a rogue ship in the ocean. Once in orbit, astronaut Jeffrey Hoffman spotted that one of Hubble's 40-foot solar arrays was bent at a 90-degree angle. During a record-setting five spacewalks conducted by two alternating astronaut teams, the crew replaced the bent solar panel—with astronaut Kathy Thornton releasing the 350-pound panel over Somalia to burn up in the atmosphere. The spacewalkers also installed corrective optics packages COSTAR and WFPC2 to fix the optical aberration in Hubble's main mirror, swapped out failing gyroscopes, upgraded the onboard computer, and even spun a dreidel for Hanukkah in space. The mission restored Hubble's sight, delivering clear deep-space images.
