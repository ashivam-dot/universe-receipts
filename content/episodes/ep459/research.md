# ep459 research: How a Hammer Failed to Save NASA's First Color Moon TV (Mission Files)

Researched 2026-11-20 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.6-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: In November 1969, Apollo 12 touched down on the Moon's Ocean of Storms, carrying the first color television camera ever brought to the lunar surface. Four months after Apollo 11's grainy black-and-white broadcast, viewers on Earth were eager to see the Moon in vivid color. But less than an hour into the first moonwalk, astronaut Alan Bean unclipped the Westinghouse camera to mount it on a tripod. Moving in his bulky suit, Bean accidentally pointed the camera lens directly into the blazing Sun. The intense solar radiation instantly scorched the camera's delicate light-sensitive tube. Seeing the broadcast drop out, Bean attempted the ultimate low-tech repair—whacking the top of the space-age camera with his geology hammer. It stayed broken, abruptly killing the live broadcast for millions of viewers. For the rest of the mission, visual records were limited to still film and Bean's vivid memories.

## Disputed or left out

- Whether the camera burn was caused by direct sunlight or high-intensity reflection off the lunar module: early on Bean suspected glare from the lander, but post-mission TV analysis confirmed the lens was pointed directly into the Sun while being carried to the tripod. Scripts should specify Bean pointed the camera toward the Sun.
