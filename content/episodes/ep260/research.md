# ep260 research: Voyager's Thirty Thousand Year Journey (Scale Breaker)

Researched 2026-10-30 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.8-flash, gemini-3.8-flash-lite-tts, gemini-3.8-flash-tts) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: At the outermost border of our Solar System lies the Oort cloud, a giant theoretical shell of icy bodies surrounding the Sun. First postulated by Ernst Öpik in 1932 and expanded by Jan Oort in 1950, this frozen reservoir sits between 2,000 and 100,000 astronomical units away, extending up to a quarter of the distance to the nearest star. Formed 4.6 billion years ago when young giant planets flung leftover planetesimals into deep space, these icy worlds are nudged inward by galactic tides and passing stars to become long-period comets. Because no telescope has ever directly observed the Oort cloud, it remains unseen. NASA's Voyager 1 spacecraft, traveling a million miles a day, will take about 300 years just to reach its inner edge and roughly 30,000 years to pass through it completely. Long before it arrives, its power supply will fail, leaving humanity to study this frozen frontier through cosmic driftwood.

## Disputed or left out

- The exact outer boundary distance varies between sources (ranging from 50,000 AU up to 200,000 AU); a script should state it extends up to 100,000 AU or state 'estimates range up to 200,000 AU'.
- Hypotheses regarding undetected companion bodies like Nemesis or Tyche driving comet showers have been disproved or lack supporting evidence; leave these out or present them strictly as past debunked hypotheses.
