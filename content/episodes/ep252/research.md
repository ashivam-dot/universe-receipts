# ep252 research: The Broken Probe That Sailed on Sunlight to Mercury (Mission Files)

Researched 2026-10-29 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.5-flash, gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.6-flash, gemini-3.8-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: In 1973, NASA launched Mariner 10 on a shoestring budget to attempt what seemed impossible: reaching Mercury using a gravitational slingshot past Venus. No mission had ever pulled off an interplanetary gravity assist. On its way, the probe suffered relentless breakdowns. Its camera heaters failed, the onboard computer repeatedly reset, and shiny flakes of peeling paint confused the star-tracker into mistaking paint specks for the guide star Canopus. When attitude control gas began running critically low, engineers improvised a historic first, angling the solar panels to sail on the pressure of sunlight itself. The gambit paid off. Venus pulled Mariner 10 onto a trajectory that slung it past Mercury not once, but three times. Its cameras beamed back images of a heavily cratered, Moon-like world and detected an unexpected intrinsic magnetic field. Running entirely out of fuel, Mariner 10 went silent in 1975, left orbiting the Sun forever.

## Disputed or left out

- Originator of the gravity assist trajectory: S1 attributes the calculations for the resonant Mercury return orbit to Giuseppe Colombo, while S4 highlights Michael A. Minovitch's pioneering computational work solving the restricted three-body problem for gravity-assisted interplanetary travel. A script should attribute both contributions appropriately.
- Magnetic field characterization: S2 and S3 characterize Mercury's magnetic field as weak or small (roughly 1/60th of Earth's), while S4 terms it 'fairly strong' relative to prior expectations of an inactive core. A script should simply state that Mercury possesses an unexpected intrinsic magnetic field.
