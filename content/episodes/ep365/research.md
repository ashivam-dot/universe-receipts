# ep365 research: The Craft That Saved Mars Exploration (Mission Files)

Researched 2026-11-06 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview, gemini-3.6-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: In May 1971, NASA launched Mariner 9 to attempt history's first orbit of another planet, just weeks after its twin Mariner 8 plunged into the Atlantic Ocean. Reaching Mars in November 1971, Mariner 9 arrived first ahead of Soviet probes, only to find the entire Red Planet blinded by a colossal, planet-wide dust storm. While Soviet craft blindly took useless photographs of dust due to fixed pre-programming, NASA controllers reprogrammed Mariner 9 from Earth to wait out the tempest. When the dust finally cleared in early 1972, Mariner 9 revealed a dynamic world: gargantuan extinct volcanoes like Olympus Mons, ancient dried riverbeds suggesting past liquid water, and a 2,500-mile canyon system named Valles Marineris in its honor. By transmitting 7,329 photos and mapping 85% of the surface, Mariner 9 transformed our understanding of Mars and paved the way for future landers.

## Disputed or left out

- Orbital entry date discrepancy: EBSCOhost (S6) claims Mariner 9 achieved orbit on September 22, 1971, whereas Wikipedia (S1), NASA (S2), Space.com (S4), and Linda Hall Library (S7) state it entered orbit on November 14, 1971. The script should use November 14, 1971.
- Mariner 9 crash/impact status: NASA and sources note predictions that Mariner 9 would crash into Mars around 2020 or 2022, but S7 notes there is no proof it actually did so yet. The script should describe it as expected to crash due to orbital decay rather than stating a confirmed crash date.
- Conclusive proof of past water: S4 and S6 state Mariner 9 provided evidence/indications that water once flowed on Mars, while S2 notes spacecraft data could not provide conclusive proof. The script should describe the findings as strong evidence or indications of ancient flowing water.
