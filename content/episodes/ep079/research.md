# ep079 research: The Cosmic Colossus That Baffles Astronomers (Scale Breaker)

Researched 2026-10-08 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.5-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: On June 19, 1790, astronomer William Herschel discovered a faint celestial smudge in the constellation Virgo. Today, we know that smudge as IC 1101, a supergiant galaxy anchored at the center of the Abell 2029 galaxy cluster over one billion light-years away from Earth. Recent ultra-deep observations using the Isaac Newton Telescope revealed that IC 1101 spans an astonishing 1.7 million light-years in diameter. If it were positioned between our Milky Way and the Andromeda Galaxy, its main stellar body would stretch almost the entire gap between them. Containing trillions of solar masses in stars, this cosmic colossus grew into a monster by cannibalizing neighboring galaxies over billions of years. Even more mind-bending, astronomers found that IC 1101 has not stopped feeding: faint plume-like remnants in its outskirts show that this cosmic giant is actively devouring nearby galactic debris and continuing to grow.

## Disputed or left out

- Earlier photographic plate measurements and different distance assumptions estimated IC 1101's outer diffuse halo at up to 6 million light-years (2 million light-years radius), but modern ultra-deep imaging defines the coherent main stellar body at 1.7 million light-years across. The script should specify that 1.7 million light-years is the robustly measured coherent stellar body, while diffuse halo material and past estimates reached larger scales.
