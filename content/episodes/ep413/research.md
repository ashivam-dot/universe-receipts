# ep413 research: The Ghost Module That Started the Space Station (Mission Files)

Researched 2026-11-13 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.6-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: On November 20, 1998, a Russian Proton rocket blasted off from Baikonur Cosmodrome carrying Zarya, the foundational first module of the International Space Station. Built in Moscow by Khrunichev but funded by NASA under an American contract, Zarya was designed to provide guidance, propulsion, and electrical power to the nascent station. Just two weeks later, Space Shuttle Endeavour launched on mission STS-88 carrying the American Unity module. High above Earth, the shuttle crew used Endeavour's robotic arm to capture Zarya and join the two modules together in orbit, while astronauts completed spacewalks to connect electrical lines. For over a year and a half, the robotic outpost flew uncrewed, autonomously maintaining its orbit. The arrival of the Russian Zvezda module in July 2000 took over primary station control, shifting Zarya's main role to cargo storage and propellant housing. Today, Zarya remains at the core of humanity's continuous laboratory in space.

## Disputed or left out

- Some commentators suggested Zarya's structural shell was built from mothballed Soviet-era hardware for the canceled Skif laser weapon program (Polyus), though this is presented as speculation; a script should attribute this as 'according to some aerospace analysts' or omit it.
- Exact timezone differences led to docking timestamps for Zvezda being recorded as July 25 (Moscow Summer Time) or July 26; a script should simply state July 2000.
