# ep373 research: The Rocket That Struck ITSELF (Near Misses)

Researched 2026-11-07 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview, gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.6-flash, gemini-3.8-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: On November 14, 1969, Apollo 12 launched into a rainy Florida sky with President Richard Nixon watching from the stands. Just 36.5 seconds into flight, a bolt of lightning struck the Saturn V rocket, followed by a second strike at 52 seconds. The spacecraft itself had triggered the bolts, acting like a giant flying lightning rod. Inside the command module, all three fuel cells disconnected, the cockpit became a blinding wall of warning lights, and Mission Control's telemetry turned into total gibberish. While the Saturn V's independent guidance system kept the rocket flying true, the spacecraft seemed doomed. In Houston, 26-year-old flight controller John Aaron recognized the telemetry failure pattern from an obscure test and called out, 'Try SCE to Aux.' Neither the flight director nor the commander knew what the switch was, but lunar module pilot Alan Bean found it, flipped it, and restored telemetry, saving the mission to the Moon.

## Disputed or left out

- Fate of the Saturn V third stage (S-IVB): S2 states that after failing to reach heliocentric orbit, the S-IVB remained in Earth orbit for 42 days before burning up in the atmosphere; S1 states it stayed in semi-stable Earth orbit until 1971, escaped into solar orbit, briefly returned in 2002 as object J002E3, and remains in solar orbit. A script should leave out the post-Apollo fate of the S-IVB.
- Context of Aaron's earlier observation: S4 attributes Aaron's prior encounter to a flight simulation, while S5 notes accounts differ between a Cape ground test and a simulation, noting Aaron remembered a spacecraft test. A script should neutrally phrase it as 'an earlier test or simulation'.
