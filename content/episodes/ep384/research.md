# ep384 research: There Are More Planets Than Stars (Scale Breaker)

Researched 2026-11-08 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview, gemini-3.5-flash, gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.8-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: When you look up at the night sky, every star visible to the naked eye belongs to our home galaxy, the Milky Way. For centuries, astronomers assumed our galaxy encompassed the entire universe. That view changed after the 1920 Great Debate between Harlow Shapley and Heber Curtis, followed by Edwin Hubble's observations proving that other galaxies exist far beyond ours. Today, we know the Milky Way is a barred spiral galaxy spanning around 100,000 light-years across, with our Sun sitting roughly 26,000 light-years from the center in the Orion Arm. At its core lies Sagittarius A*, a supermassive black hole with four million times the mass of the Sun, surrounded by a dense bulge 10,000 light-years wide. But the real scale breaker is what orbits those stars: with an estimated 100 to 400 billion stars, the galaxy contains at least 100 billion planets, meaning alien worlds match or outnumber stars across the sky.
