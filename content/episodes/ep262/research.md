# ep262 research: NASA's Nuclear Drone Is Ready to Fly (Mission Files)

Researched 2026-10-31 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.6-flash, gemini-3.7-flash, gemini-3.8-flash, gemini-3.8-flash-tts) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: NASA is launching Dragonfly, a car-sized nuclear-powered octocopter, to Saturn’s moon Titan in 2028. Titan is a 'planetary laboratory' where the air is four times denser than Earth’s and gravity is only one-seventh as strong. This unique environment makes flight so efficient that Dragonfly can leapfrog across the landscape, covering more distance in a single hop than Mars rovers manage in years. Carrying eight rotors and a plutonium-powered battery, the probe will explore organic-rich dunes and impact craters where methane falls as rain and temperatures hover at a frozen -180°C. Led by Elizabeth Turtle of JHUAPL, the mission seeks to find the prebiotic building blocks of life. By landing on its skids to drill for samples, Dragonfly will investigate whether Titan’s complex carbon chemistry once mirrored the conditions that sparked life on Earth.

## Disputed or left out

- The launch date was originally 2026 but has been delayed to July 2028; the script should use the 2028 date.
- The total project cost has increased from an original $1 billion estimate to a revised $3.35 billion lifecycle cost; attribute the higher figure to the 2024 update.
- The landing site dunes are referred to as both Shangri-La and the officially named Ahmakiq Undae; the script should use Ahmakiq Undae as the official designation.
