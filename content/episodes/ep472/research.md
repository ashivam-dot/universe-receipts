# ep472 research: How Art Pastels Revealed the True Face of Mars (Mission Files)

Researched 2026-11-21 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview, gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.6-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: In 1964, humanity set out to solve the ancient mystery of Mars. After Mariner 3 failed when its payload shroud jammed, Mariner 4 launched with a new metal fairing. On July 14–15, 1965, Mariner 4 made history as the first probe to fly by Mars, passing just 6,118 miles above the surface. Anxious JPL engineers couldn't wait hours for computer image processing, so they hand-colored a raw numerical pixel printout using art pastels. The resulting images shocked the world: instead of alien cities or lush canals, Mars was a dead, moon-like world covered in ancient craters. Mariner 4 also measured a paper-thin atmosphere and icy daytime temperatures of minus 148 degrees Fahrenheit. Two years later, while cruising silently through deep space, Mariner 4 was sandblasted by a surprise meteoroid storm—likely debris from a shattered, lost comet.

## Disputed or left out

- Final termination date of contact: S1 and S6 state communications were terminated on December 21, 1967, whereas S2 states contact was maintained until December 31, 1967. A script should attribute or state 'late December 1967'.
- Pastel color choices on the hand-colored image: S4 notes the creator bought pastels for a greyscale gradient without intentionally picking Martian red colors, while S1 simply describes a pastel set used paint-by-numbers style. Attribute individual artistic choices to Richard Grumm's account.
