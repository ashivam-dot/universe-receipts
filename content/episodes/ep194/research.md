# ep194 research: The Math Error That Lost a Mars Orbiter (Mission Files)

Researched 2026-10-19 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.8-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: In 1999, NASA lost a $125 million spacecraft due to a simple conversion error. The Mars Climate Orbiter was a 'Faster, Better, Cheaper' era probe designed to study the Martian climate. After a nine-month journey, it prepared for orbital insertion, but instead of entering its intended orbit, it disappeared. An investigation revealed a critical mismatch: the builder, Lockheed Martin, used imperial units (pound-force) for thruster data, while NASA’s navigation team expected metric units (newtons). A pound-force is 4.45 times stronger than a newton; this error pushed the probe to an altitude of only 57 kilometers—far below the 80-kilometer survival limit. The orbiter was either incinerated in the atmosphere or skipped off into deep space. Navigators had flagged the trajectory discrepancy before arrival, but their concerns were dismissed due to procedural rules, resulting in one of NASA's most expensive math mistakes.

## Disputed or left out

- The total project cost is listed as $327.6 million in Wikipedia [S1] but $285 million in PBS [S3]; the script should use the $327 million figure for the full program or $125 million for the orbiter.
- The targeted insertion altitude is cited as 226 km in [S1] but 85 to 95 miles (approx 140 km) in [S6]; the script should describe the intended orbit as significantly higher than 100 kilometers.
- Whether the error was detected: [S1] reports two navigators noticed concerns that were dismissed, while [S4] states no one detected the error; the script should attribute the ignored warnings to the investigation report findings.
