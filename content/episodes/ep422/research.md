# ep422 research: Saturn Glows With Its Own Internal Rain (Space Got Weird)

Researched 2026-11-14 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview, gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.6-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: Saturn is a massive gas giant and the sixth planet from the Sun, famous for being the only planet in our Solar System less dense than water. Deep inside, its scorching core reaches temperatures of 11,700 degrees Celsius. Remarkably, Saturn radiates 2.5 times more energy into space than it absorbs from the Sun. Scientists attribute this intense heat production to liquid helium raining down through vast layers of lower-density hydrogen. As these helium droplets descend, friction generates extreme heat, eventually forming a helium shell around a core that is 9 to 22 times the mass of Earth. This internal heat furnace powers violent global atmospheric features, including peak easterly winds driving up to 1,800 kilometers per hour, a giant storm with an eyewall at its south pole, and a mysterious persisting hexagonal wave pattern at its north pole first spotted by Voyager spacecraft.
