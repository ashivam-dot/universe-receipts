# ep487 research: How NASA Saved Curiosity With a Human Trick (Mission Files)

Researched 2026-11-22 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview, gemini-3.6-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: Launched on November 26, 2011, NASA's car-sized Curiosity rover journeyed to Mars, landing inside Gale Crater on August 6, 2012, after being lowered to the surface by a rocket-powered sky crane. Designed to investigate ancient habitability, Curiosity carries advanced scientific gear, including a sample drill at the end of its 7-foot robotic arm. Packed alongside its scientific instruments are microchips containing over 1.2 million public signatures, a presidential plaque, and wheel treads designed to imprint JPL in Morse code across Martian sand. In December 2016, a critical motor powering the drill feed mechanism failed, leaving Curiosity unable to extend its drill bit to collect rock samples. Refusing to let the mission end there, NASA engineers programmed a novel workaround: using the rover's 7-foot robotic arm itself to press the drill into rocks like a human operating a drill press. In May 2018, Curiosity successfully resumed drilling on Mars.
