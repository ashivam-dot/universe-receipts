# ep156 research: The Radioactive Clock Bolted to Voyager (Space Got Weird)

Researched 2026-10-15 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview, gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.7-flash, gemini-flash-latest) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: The Voyager Golden Record is Earth's ultimate message in a bottle, but its cover carries a 'secret' atomic signature to tell aliens exactly how old the message is. NASA electroplated ultra-pure uranium-238 onto the aluminum covers of the records aboard Voyager 1 and 2. Because uranium-238 decays at a fixed rate with a half-life of about 4.5 billion years, any civilization that finds it can calculate the time since launch by measuring the remaining isotopes. Inside, the gold-plated disks contain 116 images, greetings in 55 languages, and the songs of humpback whales. The most human inclusion is an hour of brainwaves from creative director Ann Druyan, recorded as she realized she was falling in love with Carl Sagan. While NASA censored a photo of nude humans, replacing it with a silhouette, they included a pulsar map and hydrogen diagrams to teach aliens our science. These records are built to last a billion years, carrying humanity's heartbeat across the stars.

## Disputed or left out

- The inclusion of the Beatles' song 'Here Comes the Sun' is disputed; one account says EMI blocked it due to money, while another account says it was never seriously considered.
- The exact number of images is cited as 115 in one source and 116 in others; the script should specify 116 including the calibration image.
- The number of languages for greetings is cited as 55 by most sources but 59 by one account; use 55.
- The exact half-life of uranium-238 is cited as 4.468 billion years by one source and 4.51 billion years by another; use 'approximately 4.5 billion years'.
