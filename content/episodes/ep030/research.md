# ep030 research: A Teaspoon Weighs One Billion Tons (Scale Breaker)

Researched 2026-10-03 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.5-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: In 1967, graduate student Jocelyn Bell Burnell detected a radio pulse so rhythmic she nicknamed it 'Little Green Men.' It wasn't aliens, but the first evidence of a neutron star—the ultra-dense remains of a massive star that died in a supernova. These stellar remnants pack the mass of a sun into a sphere only 12 miles wide, the size of a city. This density is so extreme that a single teaspoon of neutron star material would weigh at least one billion tons on Earth. These stars spin hundreds of times per second and generate magnetic fields trillions of times stronger than Earth's. Their gravity is so powerful that their escape velocity exceeds half the speed of light. In 2017, scientists finally 'heard' two of them collide through gravitational waves, proving these cosmic forges created the very gold and platinum found in our jewelry today.

## Disputed or left out

- The exact mass of a teaspoon of neutron star material varies by source, with estimates ranging from 10 million tons to over 5 billion tons; the script should use 'at least one billion tons' to remain conservative. The atmosphere thickness is described as millimeters to centimeters in one source and exactly four inches in another; the script should attribute this to extreme gravitational compression.
- The 1,122 Hz rotation of XTE J1739−285 is mentioned as a tentative signal that requires confirmation according to source S1.
