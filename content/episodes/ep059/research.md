# ep059 research: Why Astronauts Never Escape Earth's Gravity (Space Myths, Busted)

Researched 2026-10-06 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.5-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: Think you’ve escaped Earth’s pull on the International Space Station? Think again. While many believe space is a zero-G vacuum, the Earth’s gravitational field at the station's altitude is still nearly 90 percent as strong as it is on the ground. Astronauts don't float because gravity is gone; they float because they are in a state of perpetual free fall, plummeting toward the planet at high horizontal speeds that carry them around the curve of the Earth. This constant weightlessness takes a brutal toll on the human body. Fluid shifts toward the head, creating a puffy moon-face appearance, while muscles and bones begin to waste away. Some astronauts even experience squashed eyeballs that shift their vision toward farsightedness. From Gherman Titov’s first reported space sickness in 1961 to the Vomit Comet training flights, science proves that you can never truly run away from the gravity of home.
