# ep188 research: Are the Pillars of Creation Already Gone? (Scale Breaker)

Researched 2026-10-18 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.7-flash, gemini-3.8-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: On April 1, 1995, the Hubble Space Telescope captured one of the most iconic views in human history: the Pillars of Creation. Located roughly 6,500 light-years away in the Eagle Nebula, these immense towers of cold molecular hydrogen gas and dust span four light-years from base to tip—roughly 24 trillion miles high. Inside these columns, dense pockets nicknamed EGGs, or Evaporating Gaseous Globules, act as cosmic incubators where embryonic stars ignite. But creation comes with destruction. Intense ultraviolet radiation and stellar winds from nearby massive stars are violently sandblasting the gas away in a process called photoevaporation. In fact, infrared observations from the Spitzer Space Telescope suggested a nearby supernova blast wave might have already wiped the pillars out 6,000 years ago. Because of the vast distance light must travel, humanity won't see whether they survived or vanished for another thousand years.

## Disputed or left out

- Supernova destruction vs. gradual stellar wind erosion: Nicolas Flagey and Spitzer data suggested a supernova blast wave destroyed the pillars 6,000 years ago (visible from Earth in ~1,000 years), while Stephen Reynolds pointed out a lack of expected radio and X-ray emission and argued massive stellar winds are eroding them gradually instead. Script should attribute both interpretations clearly.
- Distance to the nebula: Some sources state 6,500 light-years while others cite 7,000 light-years. Script should state the approximate range of 6,500 to 7,000 light-years.
