# ep203 research: The Asteroid That Snuck Past Earth (Near Misses)

Researched 2026-10-21 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: In August 2020, an SUV-sized asteroid named 2020 QG zipped past Earth at a blistering 27,600 miles per hour, coming within just 1,830 miles of our planet's surface. It set the record for the closest non-impacting asteroid flyby ever recorded. The most terrifying part? Nobody saw it coming. Because the space rock approached directly from the blinding direction of the Sun, ground-based observatories were completely blind to it. The Zwicky Transient Facility at Palomar Observatory didn't even spot the object until six hours after it had already made its hair-raising pass. Earth's massive gravity field didn't just let it slide by; it violently bent the asteroid's trajectory, turning its path by roughly 45 degrees. If this 10-to-20-foot rock had actually been on a collision course, it wouldn't have caused a global apocalypse, but it would have violently disintegrated into a brilliant atmospheric fireball.
