# ep210 research: The Asteroid Sample That Was Too Big (Mission Files)

Researched 2026-10-21 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview, gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.8-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: In 2020, NASA’s OSIRIS-REx spacecraft attempted a high-stakes 'fist-bump' with the carbon-rich asteroid Bennu to collect a surface sample. Using a burst of nitrogen gas, the probe’s TAGSAM arm successfully stirred up rocks and dust, but it was too successful. The collector head grabbed so much material that large pebbles jammed the internal mylar seal open. Mission control watched in horror through onboard cameras as precious bits of the asteroid drifted away into the vacuum of space. Fearing the entire sample might leak out, NASA scrapped plans to weigh the material and rushed to stow the container in its return capsule days early. When the capsule finally landed in Utah in 2023, scientists found 121.6 grams of material—more than double their original goal. This pristine haul contained water-altered minerals and the chemical building blocks of life, including amino acids and the essential components of DNA.

## Disputed or left out

- The exact number of nucleobases found is described as 'four' by some sources (S1) and 'all 5' by others (S4); the script should refer to them generally as the essential building blocks of DNA and RNA.
- The biological model for the Egyptian deity Bennu is debated as being either a heron or a kingfisher; the script should treat this as mythological background.
