# ep457 research: The Truth Behind Apollo 14's Famous Moon Golf Shot (Space Got Weird)

Researched 2026-11-20 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.6-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: In 1971, Alan Shepard made history as the oldest person to walk on the Moon during Apollo 14. After surviving a harrowing descent to Fra Mauro where a loose solder ball nearly triggered a false computer abort, Shepard and Edgar Mitchell completed two grueling moonwalks. Dragging a two-wheeled equipment cart through undulating regolith, the pair came within thirty meters of Cone Crater's rim before exhaustion forced Mission Control to turn them back. But before leaving, Shepard unveiled a secret stunt. Attaching a modified Wilson six-iron club head to the handle of a rock sampling tool, he dropped two golf balls into the lunar dust. Restricted by his pressurized, bulky spacesuit, Shepard could only manage one-handed swings. Live on global television, he watched the second ball sail away and proclaimed it traveled miles and miles and miles. Fifty years later, digitally enhanced imagery revealed the true receipt: his cosmic drive landed just forty yards away.

## Disputed or left out

- Whether the golf club was smuggled aboard: BBC News claims Shepard smuggled the golf club head inside his spacesuit, whereas Space.com documents that Shepard cleared the stunt in advance with Manned Spacecraft Center Director Bob Gilruth on the condition that the mission went smoothly and NASA technicians assisted in construction. A script should clarify that while popular legend calls it smuggled, Shepard had cleared a secret deal with Gilruth.
- Distance of the golf shots: Shepard claimed on live broadcast that the second ball flew 'miles and miles and miles', but 2021 digital photographic analysis by Andy Saunders and the USGA proved it only traveled about 40 yards. A script should present 'miles and miles' as Shepard's exuberant exaggeration and contrast it with the true 40-yard receipt.
