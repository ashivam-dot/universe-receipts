# ep434 research: The Planet That Orbits Completely Sideways (Space Got Weird)

Researched 2026-11-17 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview, gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.6-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: In 1781, astronomer William Herschel discovered Uranus, initially mistaking it for a comet. He tried naming it after King George III, but astronomers eventually named it Uranus, making it the only planet named after a Greek god. Orbiting 2 billion miles from Earth, Uranus turned out to be an ice giant knocked completely on its side. Because of its extreme tilt and 84-Earth-year orbit around the Sun, Uranus experiences 42-year seasons of nonstop daylight followed by 42 years of continuous darkness. Methane in its atmosphere gives the planet its aquamarine color, and despite being an ice giant, it is the coldest planet in the Solar System. In January 1986, NASA's Voyager 2 made the first and only flyby of Uranus, skimming within 50,600 miles of its cloud tops. Decades later, planetary scientists have designated a return flagship probe mission to Uranus as a top priority.

## Disputed or left out

- The exact cause of Uranus's sideways tilt and missing internal heat is unproven; scientists speculate a massive protoplanet impact knocked it over and released its heat, which a script should attribute: 'according to one leading theory'.
- Whether the highly asymmetrical magnetic field observed by Voyager 2 is Uranus's normal state or an anomaly caused by high solar wind during the flyby is disputed; attribute: 'according to recent re-analysis'.
