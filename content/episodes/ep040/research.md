# ep040 research: The Day NASA Got a Ticket for Littering (Space Got Weird)

Researched 2026-10-03 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview, gemini-3.5-flash, gemini-3.5-flash-lite) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: When the United States' first space station, Skylab, was abandoned in orbit, NASA planned to use the upcoming Space Shuttle to boost and save it. However, shuttle development delays and intense solar activity caused Skylab's orbit to decay much faster than expected. On July 11, 1979, the station reentered the atmosphere, disintegrating in a massive global media event. Ground controllers tried to direct the station into the Indian Ocean, but a trajectory miscalculation sent flaming debris showering across Western Australia. In the coastal town of Esperance, pieces of space junk crashed onto properties and roofs. A local 17-year-old named Stan Thornton recovered debris and flew to San Francisco to claim a $10,000 prize offered by a newspaper. Meanwhile, the cheeky Shire of Esperance issued a real $400 littering fine to NASA. The space agency ignored the bill, but in 2009, a California radio show's listeners raised the money and paid it off.

## Disputed or left out

- Re-entry date: S3 states Skylab fell back to Earth on June 11, 1979, while S1, S5, S6, and S7 state it was July 11, 1979. The script should use July 11, 1979, as it is supported by the consensus.
- Station weight: S4 and S6 state Skylab weighed 77.5 tons, whereas S5 claims it was 88 tonnes. The script should avoid mentioning a specific weight or refer to it generally as a massive station.
- Who paid the fine: S5 states a local radio host paid the fine, whereas S6 and S7 state that California radio listeners raised the funds which the host then delivered. The script should clarify that the listeners raised the money.
