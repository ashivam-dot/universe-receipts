# ep017 research: The Spacecraft That Launched First (Mission Files)

Researched 2026-10-01 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview, gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.8-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: In 1977, NASA took advantage of a rare planetary alignment that occurs only once every 176 years. They launched Voyager 2 first, sixteen days before its twin, yet named it 'Voyager 2' because Voyager 1 was on a faster trajectory. While its twin grabbed headlines for reaching the gas giants first, Voyager 2 became the ultimate marathon runner. It remains the only spacecraft ever to visit the ice giants, Uranus and Neptune, where it discovered hidden rings and dozens of new moons. Today, the probe is over 12 billion miles away, operating on ancient technology with millions of times less memory than a modern cellphone. Because its path past Neptune sent it diving south, it can now only hear commands from a single antenna in Australia. Powered by decaying plutonium, it has officially entered interstellar space, acting as a silent ambassador for a world it left nearly half a century ago.

## Disputed or left out

- The number of moons discovered at Uranus: one source claims 11 (S1) while another says 10 (S4). Script should say 'at least 10'.
- Launch interval: one source says 16 days (S1) while another says 'within 15 days' (S4). Script should say 'roughly two weeks'.
- Alignment frequency: sources vary between 175 years (S2) and 176 years (S4). Script should say 'once every 176 years' or 'nearly 180 years'.
