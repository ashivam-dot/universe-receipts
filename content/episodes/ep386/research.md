Researched 2026-11-08 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview, gemini-3.5-flash, gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.6-flash, gemini-3.8-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: Orbiting 250 miles above Earth at five miles per second, the International Space Station is the largest human-made structure ever placed in space. Built through more than 40 assembly flights by an international partnership of 15 nations, the outpost spans 356 feet end-to-end—rivaling the size of an American football field and weighing over 900,000 pounds. Yet despite its massive footprint, its pressurized interior equals just a single Boeing 747 airliner. Since November 2000, the station has sustained continuous human habitation, with crews circling the globe every 90 minutes while conducting breakthrough research and testing hardware for future missions to Mars. However, this orbital giant cannot fly forever. Aging structural hulls, radiator leaks, and atmospheric drag mean its days are numbered. By 2030, NASA and its international partners plan to use a specialized deorbit spacecraft to steer the 400-plus-ton station into a targeted, destructive reentry over the remote Pacific Ocean.

## Disputed or left out

- Overall structural dimensions: S1 and S2 state the Integrated Truss Structure itself is 356 feet (108.5 meters) long, whereas NASA (S3) states the truss is 310 feet (94 meters) and that 356 feet is the solar array wingspan and total end-to-end station length. Scripts should describe the 356-foot figure as the station's overall end-to-end span or wingspan rather than the truss length alone.
- Exact total mass: Sources vary from approximately 410,000 kg / 900,000 lb (S2) to 925,335 lb / 419,725 kg (S3), 430 metric tons (S6), and 500 tons cited in Russian rhetoric (S1). Scripts should state 'over 900,000 pounds' or 'over 400 metric tons' rather than an exact single-digit pound figure.
- Total project cost: S2 cites a cost of around $150 billion USD, while S7 refers to it as a '$100 billion orbiting lab.' Scripts should avoid a single definitive dollar figure or attribute it as 'estimated between 100 and 150 billion dollars.'
