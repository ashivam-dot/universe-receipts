# ep049 research: The Dead Star That Can Erase Your Credit Cards (The Universe Is Terrifying)

Researched 2026-10-04 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview, gemini-3.5-flash, gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.8-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: In 1979, astronomers detected an unprecedented burst of gamma radiation that saturated spacecraft across the solar system, unveiling an extreme class of dead stars known as magnetars. These city-sized stellar remnants pack more mass than our Sun into a sphere only 12 miles across, wrapped in magnetic fields of 1,000 trillion gauss—the strongest in the known universe. A magnetar is so magnetically fierce that from halfway to the Moon, it could wipe every credit card on Earth, and venturing within 1,000 kilometers would dissolve your cellular chemistry. On December 27, 2004, a starquake on magnetar SGR 1806-20 unleashed a megaflare across 50,000 light-years. In just a tenth of a second, it released more energy than our Sun produces in over 100,000 years. The invisible gamma pulse struck Earth's ionosphere, expanding our upper atmosphere from halfway across the galaxy. Had it erupted within 10 light-years, it would have wiped out our ozone layer.

## Disputed or left out

- Distance to SGR 1806-20: S2 cites roughly 42,000 light-years (13 kiloparsecs), whereas S1, S3, S5, S6, and S7 cite approximately 50,000 light-years. Scripts should state 'around 40,000 to 50,000 light-years away'.
- Energy output comparison: S2 and S3 state the 2004 flare exceeded the Sun's output over 150,000 years, while S5, S6, and S7 state 100,000 years. Scripts should state 'more energy than our Sun produces in 100,000 years'.
- Prehistoric extinctions: Speculation that ancient terrestrial extinctions were caused by prehistoric magnetar flares is unconfirmed hypothesis; scripts should describe this as speculative.
