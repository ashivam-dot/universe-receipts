# ep460 research: The First Craft on Mars Carried Skis (Mission Files)

Researched 2026-11-20 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview, gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.6-flash, gemini-3.8-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: In May 1971, the Soviet Union launched Mars 2, a 4.6-ton spacecraft carrying humanity's boldest interplanetary gamble: an orbiter, a lander, and a rover. Tucked inside the descent capsule was PrOP-M, a miniature robotic rover designed to waddle across the Martian soil on skis while connected to its mothership by a 50-foot umbilical cable—more than 25 years before NASA deployed Sojourner. But when Mars 2 arrived on November 27, 1971, Mars was shrouded in one of the most ferocious planet-wide dust storms ever recorded. The landing craft separated and slammed into the Martian atmosphere at six kilometers per second. Due to a steep angle of entry, the landing sequence failed, the parachute never deployed properly, and the probe smashed into the ground. Mars 2 became the first human-made object to touch Mars, leaving behind a Soviet emblem and an undeployed rover, while its orbiter survived to map the dust-veiled world.

## Disputed or left out

- Parachute failure sequence: S1 and S7 state the parachute did not deploy at all, whereas S5 describes drogue and main chutes deploying but failing to brake adequately before a 500g impact. Scripts should attribute this simply as a failure of the landing and parachute system.
- Life detection capability: S1 and S7 claim the lander carried a scoop to search for organic materials and signs of life, while S5 explicitly asserts no biological or life detection experiments were carried. Scripts should leave out any life detection claims.
- Rover connection: S6 briefly calls PrOP-M an untethered rover in passing, but later affirms it was tethered by a 50-foot cable, matching S1, S2, and S7.
