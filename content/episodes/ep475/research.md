# ep475 research: Waking Voyager 1 Engines After 37 Years (Mission Files)

Researched 2026-11-21 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: In 1977, NASA launched Voyager 1 on an ambitious grand tour of the outer solar system. The spacecraft delivered historic flybys, discovering active volcanoes on Io and detailed ring structures around Saturn before pushing past the outer boundary of the heliosphere. In August 2012, Voyager 1 made history by becoming the first human-made object to cross into interstellar space. By 2017, after four decades traveling through deep space, its primary orientation thrusters were degrading. To keep the probe's antenna locked onto Earth, engineers attempted a daring test: firing a set of backup thrusters that had been completely dormant since 1980. Operating over 11 billion miles away, the 37-year-old backup thrusters fired perfectly, extending the operational lifetime of humanity's farthest interstellar scout.

## Disputed or left out

- Exact date of the 2017 thruster test: S1 states December 2017 while S2 specifies November 29, 2017. Script should attribute to late 2017.
- Exact calendar day of entering interstellar space: S2 key dates list August 1, 2012, while S1 specifies August 25, 2012. Script should state August 2012.
- Timeframe to exit the Oort Cloud: S1 estimates approximately 30,000 years to pass through, whereas S2 estimates 14,000 to 28,000 years. Script should describe it generally as thousands of years.
