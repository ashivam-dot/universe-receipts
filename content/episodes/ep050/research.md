# ep050 research: The Day an Undetected Asteroid Blindsided Earth (Near Misses)

Researched 2026-10-04 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview, gemini-3.5-flash, gemini-3.5-flash-lite) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: On February 15, 2013, astronomers were focused on asteroid 367943 Duende, which was predicted to fly safely past Earth. But they were completely blind to another danger. Out of the glare of the Sun, an undetected 10,000-ton asteroid slammed into the atmosphere over Chelyabinsk, Russia, at 19 kilometers per second. It exploded with the energy of 30 Hiroshima bombs, momentarily outshining the Sun. Dashcams captured the spectacular fireball and the rising double-plume. Although the air burst occurred 23 to 30 kilometers high, absorbing most of the energy, it generated a powerful shock wave. Minutes later, the blast shattered glass across six cities, injuring around 1,500 people. Coincidentally, the orbits of the two asteroids showed they were entirely unrelated. Scientists later discovered that the Chelyabinsk rock had experienced a complex history of cosmic collisions, and its orbit matched a much larger asteroid, suggesting it was once part of a larger companion.

## Disputed or left out

- The mass of the largest recovered fragment from Lake Chebarkul is disputed between Wikipedia articles, with S1 stating 654 kg and S2 stating 540 kg. A script should leave this specific weight out as it only appears on Wikipedia.
- The pre-entry mass of the asteroid is estimated differently across sources (S1/S2 say 9,100 tonnes, S3 says 10,000 to 11,000 metric tons, and S7 says 12,000 tons). A script should use a range like 10,000 to 12,000 tons.
- The explosive yield has been suggested to be as high as 57 megatons in S1, but S1, S4, S6, and S7 agree on a best estimate of 400 to 500 kilotons. A script should stick to the standard 400 to 500 kiloton range.
