# ep024 research: The Siberian explosion that left no crater (Near Misses)

Researched 2026-10-02 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview, gemini-3.5-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: On the morning of June 30, 1908, a blinding flash lit the skies over Siberia, followed by a shock wave that knocked people off their feet hundreds of miles away and flattened eighty million trees across an area of two thousand one hundred square kilometers. Yet, remarkably, it left behind no impact crater. For decades, scientists puzzled over the cataclysm, which released energy equivalent to thousands of atomic bombs. Because no large space rock fragments were initially found, wild theories emerged, ranging from comets to antimatter. Decades later, researchers discovered microscopic extraterrestrial spheres trapped in the tree resin and peat layers. The consensus today is that a stony asteroid exploded high in the atmosphere, creating a devastating air burst. It stands as the largest impact event in recorded human history, reminding us just how violently protective our atmosphere truly is.

## Disputed or left out

- Whether Lake Cheko is an impact crater formed by a surviving fragment of the asteroid; scripts should attribute this claim to proposing Italian researchers rather than stating it as a settled fact.
- Whether the incoming body was definitively a comet or a stony/iron asteroid; scripts should note that modern consensus leans toward an asteroid air burst, while acknowledging historical comet theories.
