# ep446 research: Why the Sun Is Not Actually on Fire (Space Myths, Busted)

Researched 2026-11-19 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview, gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.6-flash, gemini-3.8-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: For centuries, people assumed the Sun was on fire. But fire is a chemical reaction requiring oxygen, while the Sun glows through nuclear fusion. In 1920, astrophysicist Arthur Eddington correctly proposed that stars fuse hydrogen into helium. Francis Aston had discovered that four hydrogen atoms weigh slightly more than one helium atom, with that missing 0.7 percent of mass converting into pure energy via Einstein's E=mc squared. In 1934, Mark Oliphant, Paul Harteck, and Ernest Rutherford achieved artificial deuterium fusion in the lab. Soon after, Hans Bethe mapped out the Sun's proton-proton chain. Surprisingly, solar fusion is remarkably gradual: at fifteen million kelvin, the solar core generates only about 276 watts per cubic meter, less volumetric power than a resting human body. The Sun's sheer size is what lights up the solar system.

## Disputed or left out

- Peak JET fusion energy output records: S1 cites 69 MJ produced in 2023, whereas S3 cites a record of 59 MJ (attribute: 'according to different experimental reports').
- Exact solar core temperature: S1 states about 15 million kelvin while S2 specifies 15.7 million kelvin (attribute: 'estimates range between 15 and 15.7 million kelvin').
- Arthur Ruhlig's 1938 proton recoil ratio: S1 notes Ruhlig reported secondary DT neutrons, while S3 highlights that his reported 1:1000 ratio was higher than modern calculations (attribute: 'according to recent historical analysis').
