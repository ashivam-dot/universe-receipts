# ep137 research: The Spacewalker Trapped Outside His Ship (Near Misses)

Researched 2026-10-13 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview, gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.7-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: On March 18, 1965, Soviet cosmonaut Alexei Leonov made history on Voskhod 2 by performing humanity's first spacewalk. But the triumph quickly became a brush with death. In the vacuum of space, Leonov's spacesuit ballooned and stiffened so severely that his boots and gloves slipped off his limbs, leaving him unable to fit into the airlock. Facing suffocation within minutes as the capsule hurtled into orbital darkness, Leonov broke protocol by bleeding off his suit's oxygen below safety limits without alerting ground control, desperately wrestling back inside head-first. Back aboard, their guidance system failed, forcing a manual reentry burn. The orbital module failed to detach cleanly, sending the craft into a violent ten-G spin before burning free. Voskhod 2 crash-landed deep in the snow-covered Ural taiga, where Leonov and crewmate Pavel Belyayev spent two freezing nights surrounded by bears and wolves before ski rescuers arrived.

## Disputed or left out

- The exact length of the spacewalk tether varies across accounts, with some sources listing 5.35 meters (17.6 feet) and others listing 4.8 meters (16 feet); scripts should generalize or specify according to one record.
- The distance the capsule overshot its landing zone differs considerably, ranging from 386 km (240 mi) to over 600 miles or 1,500 to 2,000 km in Leonov's personal recollections; scripts should attribute the variance ('landing hundreds of miles off course').
- Accounts differ on whether the rope ladder rescue was rejected by aviation commanders as too hazardous or attempted and aborted by the cosmonauts because their stiff suits made climbing impossible; scripts should attribute both viewpoints.
