# ep251 research: The 24-Year Streak of Living Off Earth (Mission Files)

Researched 2026-10-29 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.5-flash, gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.6-flash, gemini-3.8-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: On October 31, 2000, three spacefarers—former U.S. Navy SEAL Bill Shepherd and Russian cosmonauts Yuri Gidzenko and Sergei Krikalev—launched from Baikonur aboard a Soyuz rocket. Two days later, on November 2, 2000, they docked with the International Space Station and floated inside, opening a landmark chapter in spaceflight. At the time, the station consisted of just three main modules, so power-starved that one module was left unheated. Shepherd invoked naval tradition and insisted on calling their outpost Alpha. During their 136-day stay, the trio unpacked supplies, activated life support systems, and hosted visiting space shuttles that delivered massive solar arrays and the Destiny laboratory module. By the time they handed control over to Expedition 2 and returned home aboard Space Shuttle Discovery in March 2001, they had established an unbroken streak of human orbital habitation that continues to this day.

## Disputed or left out

- Early ESA press releases (S3) targeted February 2001 for Expedition 2 to take over, whereas actual handover and return occurred in March 2001 (S1, S5, S7); scripts should state the actual March 2001 handover.
- S1 notes Russian space agency President Yuriy Semenov disapproved of the name Alpha preferring Beta or Mir 2, while Shepherd insisted on Alpha; scripts should present Alpha as an informal call sign used by the crew.
