# ep367 research: The Moons That Swap Orbits Every Four Years (Space Got Weird)

Researched 2026-11-06 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.6-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: In 1966, astronomers looking at Saturn spotted a small moon near its rings, naming it Janus. But their tracking data made no sense: the object's calculated position kept shifting unexpectedly. Twelve years later, scientists solved the mystery. Astronomers hadn't been tracking one object, but two distinct moons, Janus and Epimetheus, sharing almost the exact same orbit around Saturn, separated by only 50 kilometers. According to orbital dynamics, two bodies so close should eventually collide. But instead of crashing, Janus and Epimetheus engage in an unprecedented cosmic dance. Every four years, as the inner, faster moon catches up to the outer one, their mutual gravity forces them to trade angular momentum. The inner moon rises outward and slows down, while the outer moon falls inward and speeds up, swapping places without ever colliding. Confirmed by Voyager 1 and Cassini, it remains the only known orbital swap of its kind in the Solar System.

## Disputed or left out

- Minimum distance during orbital swap: Wikipedia lists ~10,000 km, whereas NASA Science and Planetary Society list ~15,000 km (9,000 miles). Script should state 'between 10,000 and 15,000 kilometers'.
- Origin of the month January: Popular tradition credits the month to Janus, but source S5 states the first month was actually named after Juno. Script should leave out this etymological point to avoid confusion.
