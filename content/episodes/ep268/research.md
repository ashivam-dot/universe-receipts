# ep268 research: The Planet That Almost Became a King (Space Got Weird)

Researched 2026-10-31 by the cloud studio (gemini-3-flash-preview, gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.6-flash, gemini-3.7-flash, gemini-3.8-flash-tts) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: In March 1781, German-born musician and amateur astronomer William Herschel was scanning the night sky from his home garden in Bath, England, using a homemade reflecting telescope. He spotted a faint, disk-like object moving slowly against the background stars. At first, Herschel assumed he had discovered a new comet. However, orbit calculations by fellow astronomers soon proved the object moved in a nearly circular path around the Sun, revealing it as the first planet discovered since antiquity. By uncovering a world orbiting twice as far out as Saturn, Herschel instantly doubled the known size of the Solar System. Eager to honor his royal patron, Herschel proposed naming the seventh planet 'Georgium Sidus' (George's Star) after King George III. While King George rewarded him with an annual stipend, international astronomers strongly rejected naming a planet after a British monarch. Ultimately, German astronomer Johann Elert Bode's suggestion took hold, officially naming the ice giant Uranus.
