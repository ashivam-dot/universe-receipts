# ep105 research: The Storm That Bit Its Own Tail (Scale Breaker)

Researched 2026-10-09 by the cloud studio (gemini-3.5-flash) from the sources below, read in full by the studio. Only claims backed by two or more independent sites were allowed into the script.

Story: Every 20 to 30 years—roughly once a Saturnian year—Saturn unleashes a colossal megastorm known as a Great White Spot. First cataloged in 1876, six of these planetary tempests have erupted over the past 140 years, alternating between the equator and mid-latitudes. When a storm erupts, a thunderous head as wide as planet Earth generates up to ten lightning strikes per second. High-altitude winds whip the storm into a turbulent tail that stretches tens of thousands of kilometers around the gas giant until it literally collides with its own head, completely encircling the planet. Planetary scientists discovered that the storms are bottled up for decades because heavy water vapor precipitates out and suppresses atmospheric convection until the cooling upper layers catastrophically destabilize. Long after the clouds vanish, radio observations reveal that chemical scars from these colossal upheavals linger inside Saturn's atmosphere for centuries.
